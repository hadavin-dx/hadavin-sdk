# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Upgrade steps
- **Approval cards mask identifiers outside code.** A card is the text a person reads in a chat channel before approving an action. An e-mail address, a host name or another identifier outside code was shown with brackets in its dots; it is now a placeholder (`<EMAIL_1>`). A target system or an input shaped like a host name is shown as `<HOSTNAME_n>`: the approver cannot read it on the card; numbers are local to one card. What to do: name targets by a label that is not a host name (`orders-db`), or pass `target_labels={"db01.corp.internal": "orders-db"}` to `build(...)` (or to `Approvals`): the card then shows your label in place of the target system, and a label that is itself shaped like a host name is masked like any text. Also visible: a line whose only change is a bracketed domain or a masked identifier keeps its block marker (heading, quote, list; before, it was neutralised with a zero-width space); dots in names after `/` are bracketed (`/var/log/app[.]log`); `@here`, `@channel`, `@everyone` right after a masked value read `at-here`. Summaries stored before the upgrade keep their old text, including any e-mail address in it (an exposure this change cannot undo; delete or re-issue old requests if that matters to you).
- **Redaction, gate fix: the speed tests of the Q8b files no longer depend on how busy the machine is.** A mutant (M257) that makes a rule read the text again and again was caught when it ran alone but not once when the machine was busy, and two good-code speed tests failed in a full run (the larger text took 9.8 and 10 times as long as 4 times smaller one, the bound is 6 to 9). The cause: the tests read the wall clock, which also counts the time the process waits for a free core, and a CPU-time clock still counts more when other jobs share the cache and the cores. What changed: every speed test of the Q8b files reads `time.process_time()` (CPU time of the test process; `tests/test_q8b_cpu_clock.py` fails if a Q8b test reads a wall clock again) and measures the small and the large size in turns with the garbage collector off, keeping the least time of each (`measure_ratio` in `tests/q8b_helpers.py`). A real slowdown has a ratio of 16 or more and fails every turn. Measured with four busy-loop processes alongside and a machine load of 9 to 11 on 4 cores: the 26 speed tests passed in 5 of 5 runs; the ratios of the large to the small size were 3.87 to 4.34 for 22 measurements and 5.60 for one (bounds 6 to 9).

- **K5-core, the scrub of a tool result.** The limits below are pinned by tests in `tests/test_k5_fixes5.py` to `test_k5_fixes9.py` and `tests/test_k5_layers.py`; the two figures on the end of a cut text (the proportion that loses a group) are estimates from a run, not pinned.
  - **Size.** Scrubbing grows a short secret (6 characters become the 17-character `<SECRET_REDACTED>`), so a result far below the 262,144-character cap could pass it and was refused (`InputTooLarge`; 15,421 copies of a 6-character secret was the smallest case).   - **Cuts.** A cut can split a handed-out secret (the raw-size cut, the window cut, the cut after a replace inside the scrub), and then no whole spelling is left to mask. After EVERY such cut, and before any swap of the text for its decoded view, the scrub removes: a half-written escape at the end (`\u005`, `&#9`, `%C3`, half of a surrogate pair); the longest stretch before the marker that reads (layers and NFKC undone) as the start of a spelling of any handed-out secret (a compatibility-character spelling can need more than one character to read as a start) (hex in any case); and the front of a base64 token that wraps a secret (standard or URL-safe alphabet, token of 8 characters or more, cut back to the 4-character group that holds the first byte). A reply loses the last few characters to this; ordinary text at the very end can lose a trailing `&T`, a `%C3%A9`, or (about 3 in 100, when the tail looks like base64) its last group. The look-back is at most `evidence.scrub.fragment_scan` (4,096) start positions with one decode each.
  - **Exception messages.** The message of a tool's exception is scrubbed in full BEFORE it is cut to 160 characters (200 for a refusal), so the cut cannot leave the front of a secret.
  - **What the scrub undoes.** A handed-out secret (6 characters or more) is found as it is, URL-quoted, hex (any case), base64 and base32 (base64 also two or three times), and behind JSON string escapes (`\uXXXX` with surrogate pairs joined, `\/`, `\"`, `\\`, `\n`, `\t`; PHP writes every slash as `\/`), percent-encoding (twice), HTML entities, NFKC compatibility characters (full-width, mathematical, circled, superscript) and invisible characters. The layers are undone to a fixed point, at most 8 rounds (`defaults.SCRUB_MAX_LAYERS`; deeper is cut or replaced, see the next entry), so they may nest either way (`&#37;41`, a double-escaped entity). The redactor's second pass (`guard/runmap.py`) joins surrogate pairs and reads `\/` too.
  - **What it does not undo.** Hex of a percent-encoded value, ROT13 or any substitution cipher, reversed text, a character separator (`V-h-5`), one- and two-digit octal escapes, named escapes, a base32 token that wraps a secret, a base64 token shorter than 8 characters (`tools.B64_MIN_TOKEN`, the one bound), and any layer not listed: the text stays as it came and a cut inside such a spelling leaves its first characters next to the marker. This is a limit, not a guarantee: the control is that a tool is not handed a secret it does not need (least privilege). `tests/test_k5_fixes7.py` pins the unhandled layers so a change is seen.
  - **Settings.** The thresholds are validated configuration under `evidence.scrub` (`tail_window` 256, `min_redaction_window` 4096, `min_secret` 6, `fragment_scan` 4096, `fragment_min` 1, `partial_end` 10; ranges in `config.ScrubSettings`), one set of defaults in `hadavin_sdk/defaults.py`.
- **K5-core, layered encodings.** Every tool result, whether or not a secret was handed out, also gets a decode-to-fixed-point view (percent, entity, backslash escapes, NFKC) that is redacted too, so a pattern-shaped secret (`%41KIA...`, `password%3Dx`) is found; a text nested deeper than the cap is not shown. The scrub undoes any ordered stack of percent, HTML entity (decimal and hex), backslash-u, backslash-slash and NFKC full-width layers, and a base64 token front, to a fixed point: each round applies NFKC, JSON escapes, percent-decoding and entity-decoding ONE AT A TIME (a combined step turned the half entity `&#10`, which percent-decoding had just exposed, into a newline, so `percent(entity(secret))` cut inside the token left its front: 7 of 451 cut points). ### Changed
- **Fixed: a value under a credential key was shown partly masked** when it held a space, a tab, a line break, an escape character, `;`, `,`, `&` or a quote mark. It is now shown only as placeholders, or as `<SECRET_REDACTED>`.
- **Masked where repeated, with limits** (the same text as `k1b.redaction.LIMITS`, which a test compares with the code).
  The redactor is pattern based. It does not catch names in free text, customer or project names, ticket numbers, values in unusual formats, or anything else no pattern matches.
  - A key that names a credential (for example `password` or `api_key`) and every entry of a Kubernetes Secret (a stored credential record) are masked under their own key. Three things are shown as written: a text that is exactly a UTC time, a true, false or null value, and a setting about a credential (below).
  - The same value is also masked where it is repeated elsewhere in the case only when all of this holds: it is a text of 4 to 255 characters directly under that key; it is made of printable ASCII characters and white space; it does not hold both kinds of quote mark; no part of it is something another pattern masks (an e-mail address, a host name, a key); and the copy is spelled the same way.
  - An entry of a Secret under a key that does not name a credential is masked elsewhere only when it also has at least 8 characters and is not all digits or one lower-case word (a value such as `orders` stays readable where it is repeated). At most 256 texts of one Secret, nested ones included, are treated this way.
  - Not masked where repeated: longer or shorter values, numbers, values nested deeper under a credential key, copies in another spelling (base64, capitals, wrapped over lines), and values with control or other special characters (a copy of such a value may be shown partly masked).
  - A setting about a credential (a key that ends in enabled, policy, count, expiry and the like) is shown only when its value is a yes or no word, a number or a time.
  - A record key with a word that ends in `pass`, `auth` or `session` (`newpass`, `postgresauth`) is masked unless that whole word is an ordinary English word from a short list (`bypass`, `compass`, `encompass`, `overpass`, `possession`, `obsession` and the like). An ordinary word with a second word of its own family is masked, written with `_`, `-` or nothing between: after `pass` words (`compass_key`, `compass_code`, `compass_value`, `compass_phrase`, `bypass_key`) and after `session` words (`possession_id`, `possession_key`, `possession_cookie`, `obsession_id`); `protection-bypass` is masked (a bypass secret). One table serves the key=value rule, the record walk and the flag rule. A bare `bypass`, `compass` or `possession` (and the other bare ordinary words of the list) is shown in every written form; a name made of an ordinary word and MORE characters (`compass_direction`, `compass2key`) is masked when its value is in quotes (`"compass_direction": "v"`, `compass2key="v"`), as before batch 11, and is shown as an unquoted `key=value` (`compass_direction=v`, `compass2key=v`) unless the name is one of the listed pairs (`compass_key=v` is masked); whether a bare `bypass` should be masked is the owner question `q-q8b-bare-bypass` (built: shown). `oauth`, `oauth_scopes` and `oauthProvider` are masked: `oauth` may hold a token. A harmless word that ends that way and is not on the list is hidden too, although it holds no credential. The record rule also masks some other keys that hold no credential (`session_duration`, `auth_method`, `pass_rate`, `tokenCount`, `tokenType`). A key written in capitals (`COOKIEJAR`, `COOKIESTORE`) is masked as a record key, as in a `key=value`; in lower case (`cookiejar`) it is shown on both sides.
  - A flag that names a credential (`--password`, `--newpass`, `--dbpass`, `--oauth`) takes the one word after a space as its value, on a card, in a tool result and in a log line: in `--newpass a b` the word `a` is masked and `b` is shown, because `b` may be a file name or another argument of the command (this second word is shown after every credential flag: a stated design choice). After the flags `--authorization` and `--bearer` the scheme word and the token after it are both masked (any scheme word, quoted or not; a Digest value to the next flag or the end of the line); in `Authorization: Token <t>` the scheme word stays readable and the token is masked. A second word after any other credential flag is shown (`--pass2 a b`: this holds for every flag named here); `authorization=a b` is a `key=value`, not a flag: its first word is masked and `b` is shown, not a flag rule. The flags `--cookie`, `--set-cookie` and `--session-cookie` take a value; `--no-cookies`, `--load-cookies`, `--save-cookies`, `--keep-session-cookies`, `--junk-session-cookies` and `--cookies` are not known to take a credential (switches or file names), so the word after them is shown. Written `--newpass=a b`, both words are masked. A flag with seven or more words joined by `-` or `_` in front of the credential word (`--a-b-c-d-e-f-g-password v`) keeps its value readable: the flag rule reads at most six words in front. In a `key=value` the key rule reads any run of digits after a key word (`pass` and 40 digits are masked) and then at most 20 more key characters, so a key word followed by digits and more than 20 other key characters (letters, `_`, `-`, `.`) keeps its value readable there. A header wrapped over lines: after `Basic`, `Token`, `ApiKey`, `Digest` or `Bearer` in an `Authorization` header (or a `Proxy-Authorization` one), or after a credential key (`X-Api-Key`, `token`, `secret`, `passwd`) with one of these scheme words, any line break and white space is a gap, as in a single line, and the next word of 8 or more characters (any letters, digits, `.`, `~`, `:`, `-`) is read as the token; a line that starts with a word of fewer than 8 characters is shown, and a line of prose that starts with a word of 8 or more characters is masked too (cost). A bare `Basic<LF>` uses only the base64 letters and 8 characters, a bare `Bearer` needs 12, and the other schemes have no rule without a header name in front of them (as on main). A header folded over several lines (each next line starts with a space or a tab, a line fold, RFC 9112 section 5.2) is masked to its last folded line for all five schemes, also when its first line has fewer than 8 characters (`Digest u="a",<LF> response="<hash>"`). A header named `Authorization` whose first word is any other scheme word (`NTLM`, `Negotiate`, `OAuth`, `Splunk`, `AWS4-HMAC-SHA256`, a name that starts with a capital letter and has hyphens or digits), or no scheme word, is masked to its last folded line too, with up to 8 more words on its first line; a fold may also start with a vertical tab, a form feed, a next-line, a line-separator or a paragraph-separator character. A lower-case word with a hyphen after `Authorization:` is read as the token, not as a scheme word. Three limits: a line that does not start with a space or a tab ends the header, so a continuation written with no indent is shown (it cannot be told from a new header line); the folded lines have no upper bound, so an unrelated indented block right after the header is masked (cost); and for `Basic`, `Token`, `ApiKey` and `Bearer` the token ends at white space on its line (a token has no space), so a second word on the same line is shown, as after a credential flag (a `Digest` value is masked to the end of its line). After `--authorization` and a scheme word a quoted text is read to 512 characters; a longer one is left to the other rules (a long text of ordinary words is shown).
  - A header folded over several lines (`Authorization: Bearer <a>`, then lines that start with a space or a tab) is masked to its last folded line for the scheme words Basic, Token, ApiKey, Bearer and Digest, and for any other first word with up to 8 more words on the first line (a licence-style hyphenated key, `AWS4-HMAC-SHA256`). For a first word that is none of the five, the folded lines stop at a line that looks like `name:` (the next entry of pretty-printed JSON stays readable), so a folded secret that itself looks like `name:value` is shown there. A secret cut on the SAME line, or on an unindented next line, behind a scheme word of another kind is masked only as far as `main` masked it (one word); the exact reach is in the CHANGELOG. A hyphenated word after `Authorization` is masked as on `main`; behind `AWS4-HMAC-SHA256`-like words the next word on the line is masked too, and the words after it on the line are shown. The line breaks of `str.splitlines` (vertical tab, form feed, the three information separators, U+0085, U+2028, U+2029) count as line breaks of a folded header: on a card they are written as a line feed ONLY when a space or a tab follows (otherwise the card shows one space, and the redactor reads the text before and after as one line, which it masks as such), and inside a folded line they do not end the masked part (a line of text right after one is masked up to the next line feed too).
  - A bare ordinary word is shown only when NO character that can be part of a key stands in front of it: `x--bypass`, `-bypass=v`, `--bypass=v`, `x_bypass` and `new-compass` are credential keys as on `main`. The card text and the record walk read a prefix differently in four shapes, `x-bypass`, `x--bypass`, `new_bypass` and `x_compass`: the card rule sees one key (masked), the record walk cuts the key at `-` and `_` into the words `x` and `bypass` (shown). Both sides equal `main` there; the card, the log and the tool result are the paths a value reaches a person or a model through, so they mask.
  Masking is a first filter, not proof that no client data reached the model. (A sweep of the registered secrets before the patterns, which lifts most of these limits, is follow-up Q8d.)
- **Fixed on cards:** a domain that ends up outside code after masking is bracketed; masking no longer creates a mention. Limits, same entry: masking is pattern based; an address written with a wide dot (`。`) inside a code fence or inside inline code is still shown.
