
### Changed (G10: a CPU budget for the injection read and for the fold scan's future consumer, the published false-hit rates; ORDER 023, escalation E11)
- **The injection read stops at a CPU budget; the fold scan takes one for its first consumer (`guard/textnorm.py`, `guard/injection.py`, `defaults.py`).** `textnorm.scan_holds(*, text, value, budget_s=None)` and `textnorm.scan_views_no_value(raw, *, budget_s=None)` take a budget in CPU seconds; the call reads `time.thread_time()` once at its start, on the thread that runs it, and then at fixed points between whole passes (never inside one: NFKD and NFKC run over the whole text in one call); at or past start plus budget they raise the new `textnorm.DeadlinePassed` (a `TextnormError`, NOT a `TextTooLong`, so the chunked injection read does not start piece work after the deadline; `textnorm` now has 37 public names). With no budget (the default) no clock is read and every result is what it was. `RegexDetector.check` takes its budget from `defaults.INJECTION_BUDGET_S` (30.0 CPU seconds per check, not per run; None: no budget; a value that is not a positive finite number raises `ValueError` and flags every run); a check past it is flagged `guard_unavailable` (fail closed: the rest of that text is not read for that run). No redaction path calls `scan_holds` yet, and the redactor is unchanged (its own deadline): this item bounds the injection read only; the first consumer of `scan_holds` passes its own budget and converts `DeadlinePassed` into its own refusal. How late a refusal comes is the longest stretch between two readings, MEASURED on the build box on named texts only (G10 ticket, section 12; maxima of those texts, not a bound for every text): at most 0.63 s for one `scan_holds` call on a 236,879-character text of distinct letters (the size rule of a text seen for the first time), at most 0.24 s on 262,144-character texts of Devanagari words with spacing marks and of tag characters, and at most 0.26 s for the injection read of 2,000,000 characters over ten texts (among them texts dense with `ignore all` and a text whose pieces are halved). The work on a protected VALUE has no reading inside it (7.2 to 7.5 s measured for a value of 236,879 distinct letters). A constructed text of 2,000,000 characters (runs of U+FDFA, whose pieces are halved) took 185 s of CPU without the budget and was flagged at 30.1 s with it. CPU only: waiting for the GIL, a lock or I/O does not count, no call site of the injection check has a wall-clock limit, and a slower machine flags a text a faster one reads.
- **False hits of the fold scan, measured (E11; `handover/core-c/evidence/G1-false-hits-and-cost.md`, run 1 and "Round 2", 2026-11-20, Python 3.13.18, Unicode 15.1.0).** Unit: the share of scans in which `scan_holds` reports a value in a 10,000-character chunk of ordinary Latin text (repository prose and code, standard-library docstrings, generated text) that was drawn at random (random pronounceable strings and a 70-name hand list, not real protected values). Round 2: 4 letters 5.3 %, 5 letters 1.0 %, 6 letters 0.4 %, 8 letters or more 0; a common word of 5 to 10 letters 3.9 to 4.5 % (its draw is hash-seeded; two runs); ASCII ids of 4 to 5 characters 0.22 %; 6 digits 0.007 % (run 1 on an older tree: 5.2, 0.8, 0.25, 0, 4.1, 0.2, 0.005). An upper bound in one sense only: a value that is a whole word of the text counts as a hit. It is NOT a bound for other registers (tickets, email, chat) or for real protected values. Pooled over the six rows (21,590 located hits; one more was not located), 33 % of the hits are a whole word, 33 % inside a longer word (`Mary` in `summary`), 34 % across a space or punctuation (`ab12` in `ab 12`); the mix differs by row: the hits that cut a word are 94 % for 4 letters, 63 % for 5 letters, 99 % for ids of 4 to 5 characters and 9 % for 6-letter names (91 % are the whole name). Non-Latin: one program's real interface text (Russian, 4 to 8 letters, 7.6 %; Chinese, 2 characters drawn by frequency, 5.2 %, 3 to 4 characters 0); Arabic and Devanagari on synthetic text only, real rates unknown. A false hit is the safe side (more masking, or a refusal), never a leak. The "near 1%" of the insertion comment in `defaults.py` is only what the one-inserted-letter match adds; the comment now says so and gives these rates.
