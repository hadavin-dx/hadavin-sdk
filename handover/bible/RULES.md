# Hadavin FDE Rule Book

These are the rules every Hadavin project and demo follows. Source = the FDE course, distilled from the 44 topic files in `bible/topics/`. For diagrams and flows the course **images** win over the `.md` text; for everything else the notes are the authority. Course numbers are quoted exactly. Lab model names, prices and thresholds are course examples: copy the principle, and map the value with the [Hadavin practice] rules. A rule marked **[Hadavin practice]** is studio practice, not course text. `(old N)` marks a rule carried over from the previous 33-rule set in `fde-playbook/RULES.md`. "Source: topic NN Lx/Qx/Tx" points to the lesson, quiz or task in that topic file. "N:1234" points to line 1234 of the course notes file `Geeta or Bible for claude FDE\AI Forward Deployed Engineer - Notes.md`.

## 0 · Always

**R1.** Run every engagement through the four responsibilities in order: find the problem, build fast, prove it works, make it survive. Source: topic 01 L1 (image).

**R2.** Move through the four stages in fixed order (discovery, prototype, pilot, hardening and handoff), and leave a stage only when its exit artifact exists. Source: topic 02 L1, L2 (images).

**R3.** Never rush discovery or hardening, the two stages people most want to skip. When pushed to skip ahead, answer "Happy to move on once we have X." Source: topic 02 L1, L2.

**R4.** Use each stage as a cheap exit: no good use case, stop after discovery (weeks 1–2 of 12); bad pilot numbers, stop before hardening. Source: topic 02 L1.

**R5.** [Hadavin practice] Compress the calendar and run deals in parallel, but never skip a stage or its exit artifact. Each exit is a go/no-go point. Payments follow the order's payment terms (`..\egm\pricing\pricing-plan.md` §3.5), not every stage exit. A no-go at an exit ends the engagement at the price paid so far; the one payment schedule, the Week-1 Fee that must buy a deliverable the client keeps, and the 10% kept if the studio stops on an infeasible target are in `..\memory\decisions-2026-11-01-evening.md` item 5 (owner-delegated, adopted unless the owner objects). Source: topic 02, 44 real world. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A17: failed-pilot exit rule points to the owner decision)*

**R6.** Never accept "it looks good" or "it felt right" as proof. Show a measured number you can rerun tomorrow and get a comparable result. Source: topic 01 L1; 25 L1.

**R7.** Never judge an answer by how confident it sounds: fluent wrong answers get trusted, unsure ones get checked. Source: topic 03 L3.

**R8.** Tell customers plainly that the model is frozen, learns nothing from use, knows their systems only through the prompt, and can be wrong. Never promise a system that cannot be wrong. Source: topic 03 L2, L3. (old 22)

**R9.** Write down every failing question and add it to the test set: "That list is gold." Source: topic 01 L3; 26 L1; 40 L2.

**R10.** When the model is weak, first fix what you control (retrieval, instructions), then report the remaining gap with concrete failing examples. Never retrain it on your own as a quick fix to a field complaint, and never pause the work; fine-tuning is a separate decision made only under R284 to R289. Source: topic 01 Q2. (old 32) *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A8: separates the no-quick-retrain trap from the fine-tune decision tree)*

**R11.** Choose the simplest design that works: a chain when the steps are known, RAG for missing knowledge, prompting for behavior, and fine-tuning only after evals prove prompting has plateaued. Source: topic 20 L2; 30 L2. (old 19)

**R12.** Show the client's security engineer the data flow early and get their agreement; without that approval nothing ships. Source: topic 01 L3; 36 L1.

**R13.** Count values from the data; never type them by hand ("the numbers must be counted, not typed"). Source: topic 05 T2, T3.

**R14.** Never put a secret in code, a prompt, a tool result, a log or a trace. The course reads keys from environment variables. Source: topic 06 L2; 23 L2. (old 12)

**R15.** [Hadavin practice] Reach secrets only through the secrets kind (a named lookup), fetched by the workload's identity. Neutral default: environment variables injected at runtime by the deploy system (code reads `os.environ`, as in the course) or a self-hostable vault; adapters: AWS SSM or Secrets Manager, Azure Key Vault, GCP Secret Manager. Env files are never committed to a repo. Source: topic 23 real world. (old 12) *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A6, E2, B10, G26: secrets kind with a neutral default; strengthens, does not weaken, the secrets rule)*

**R16.** [Hadavin practice] Run Claude in the client's own cloud, after the client has chosen (R446): Bedrock is the recommended option on AWS, Foundry on Azure, Vertex AI on GCP, and the direct API only on request. Tiers: Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, and the default is the tier the workload chart gives (R278, R279): Sonnet 5.5 is the usual result for hard work, Haiku 5.5 for easy or high-volume work; model ids, prices and per-cloud availability live in config and are re-verified live and dated before every quote. The model is a kind (R362): Claude is the recommended default, offered as an option the client chooses (R446), and a second backend (an open-weight model on client GPUs, or another vendor on the client's cloud) is at least outlined with its trigger (outage, deprecation with no acceptable successor, price change, client policy, air-gap). It counts as an alternative only after the golden set passes on it (R180), and swapping needs only a config change plus that green run. Confirm model access for the pinned ids in the client account before the pilot (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 had per-model Bedrock access criteria on 2026-11-01) and keep a fallback id in config (open to all Bedrock customers on 2026-11-01: Sonnet 5, Opus 4.8, Haiku 4.5, Fable 5 and 5.1; Opus 5 is not open) [research 2026-11-01: platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-amazon-bedrock, "open to all Amazon Bedrock customers"]. Haiku 5.5's price steps up above 100,000 prompt tokens, so cost models branch on prompt length (ROLES carries the numbers). Source: brief; topic 07 real world; [research 2026-11-01: platform.claude.com Bedrock, models overview and pricing pages, via audit M]. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A1, A7, B1, L-11, L-20, M2, M6, D-19, I-23: model kind with alternative and trigger, access check, fallback id, verify-live stamp)* *(changed 2026-11-01 round 2: open fallback ids)*

**R17.** [Hadavin practice] Keep model ids, prices, thresholds, top-k and limits in config. Put no model, env, region or account literals in code. Source: brief; topic 07, 14, 25 real world.

**R18.** [Hadavin practice] Ship typed Python packages (uv, ruff, pytest) with modules for config, adapters (one interface plus backends), core, evals and CLI/API, plus structured logs, OpenTelemetry traces and CI gates on every PR. Source: brief; topic 05 real world.

**R19.** [Hadavin practice] Label each piece of work with its hat: advise (architect), model layer (ML engineer) or ship (FDE). Source: topic 01 L2, real world.

## 1 · Discovery & scoping

**R20.** Collect the wish list (expect about 15 ideas within an hour). Turn "add AI to everything" into one painful, measurable problem, and offer short team interviews this week. Do not stall on a requirements document and do not lecture the sponsor about the technology. Source: topic 37 L1; 01 Q1. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A10: adds the two dropped quiz anti-patterns)*

**R21.** Filter every idea with three questions, in order: painful? data reachable? measurable win? Pass all three = candidate. Fail one = parking lot, politely, with a written reason. Source: topic 37 L1 (image). (old 2)

**R22.** Ask "painful?" first. Someone must lose real hours or money every week, or nobody will fight for the project at budget review. Source: topic 37 L1.

**R23.** Confirm data access on day one: where the data lives and who grants access. An assistant over data you cannot access "is a slide, not a product". Source: topic 37 L1.

**R24.** [Hadavin practice] Count "data reachable" as proven only with a least-privilege service account or a sample export in hand before scoping closes. Source: topic 37 real world.

**R25.** State the win as a number, e.g. "cut average ticket handling time from 20 minutes to 8". No success number means discovery is not done. Pair it with a ceiling number on the worst failure (R294). Source: topic 37 L1; 02 L2. (old 1) *(changed 2026-11-01: pairs the success number with a ceiling number)*

**R26.** Build one use case first. Prefer boring, reachable and measurable over the flashiest idea, and internal users over a public audience: first projects exist to build trust. Source: topic 37 L1, Q1.

**R27.** Screen out the four bad-fit shapes: no tolerance for error, no data, no measurable outcome, cheaper without AI. For the last one, recommend the database query or form instead. Source: topic 37 L2.

**R28.** Never let an LLM own a decision where one wrong answer means a lawsuit or a regulatory fine. Reshape it: the AI drafts with cited evidence and a qualified human decides. Source: topic 37 L2, Q2.

**R29.** Decline with the three-move "no": name the real risk in business terms, reshape into a safe version, and offer a path back with real numbers. Never say "that will not work". Source: topic 37 L2, Q2. (old 2)

**R30.** Kill a doomed use case early with the customer's own evidence, and propose the one their teams asked for. Never switch quietly, and never build the doomed idea anyway. Source: topic 02 Q2.

**R31.** Keep ideas with unconfirmed data ("somewhere in the BI system") or vague wins ("better visibility") out of the first build. Source: topic 37 Q3.

**R32.** Write the scoping document before any code with these four H2s, spelled exactly: Use case; Out of scope (≥2 rejected ideas with reasons); Success metric (a number from the brief); Risks. Use real sentences. Source: topic 37 T1.

**R33.** Make sure the discovery output, one page, names the problem, who has it, where the data lives and the success number. Source: topic 02 L2 (image).

**R34.** When the brief uses the final-project template, give `scoping.md` the H2s `## Problem`, `## Users`, `## Success criteria` (numeric) and `## Risks`. Source: topic 44 T1.

**R35.** [Hadavin practice] Make `scoping.md` the proposal's scope annex, with the fixed price pinned to it. It carries both H2 sets plus target cloud, data sources with owners, calendar, the pilot plan and sponsor sign-off. No build starts without sign-off. Source: topic 37, 44 real world; 44 table.

**R36.** Ask about permissions in week one, before the first connector runs. Source: topic 19 L1.

**R37.** Ask for real sample documents in week one, always: the sales demo never shows the messy files. Source: topic 16 L3. (old 30)

**R38.** Map every knowledge source to one of four homes (databases, SaaS apps, file exports, document piles), and ask for the smallest useful source first. Source: topic 24 L1 (image).

**R39.** Ask early for the customer's id formats (codes, tickets, accounts). Redaction regexes and keyword search are only as good as your knowledge of the shapes. Source: topic 36 L3; 17 L1.

**R40.** Classify the data touched against the four project-stopping categories: PII, credentials/secrets, regulated data, trade secrets. Source: topic 36 L1.

**R41.** Bring the data-flow answer to the security meeting: what enters a prompt, what is stripped before it leaves, and the log that proves it. Source: topic 36 L1 (image).

**R42.** Map data class to deployment: public/internal → SaaS API, regulated → VPC, air-gap → on-prem, full stop. Never walk in selling one option. State the trade-offs in the options list (course, N:14022-14060): SaaS is the easiest and least control, accepted for non-sensitive work especially when the contract gives no-training and data-retention terms; VPC costs more, has a smaller model choice, and someone must operate it; on-prem is usually open-weight models only, needs GPU hardware, and every upgrade is a manual project. A common landing is a split: pilot on the SaaS API with strict redaction, move to VPC for production if the data class demands it. Source: topic 36 L2 (image). *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read A22)*

**R43.** Answer "will our data train the model?" with the fact (API traffic isn't used for training by default) plus the provider's data processing terms, read beforehand. Re-check live which terms govern each channel (the client-cloud model service, the direct commercial API, and the studio's own Claude Code account, consumer or commercial); never put client data in a channel whose terms allow training or long retention (R360). Source: topic 36 Q1. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit G2: channel-by-channel terms check)*

**R44.** [Hadavin practice] Default to Claude through the client's own cloud account and region over a private network path (PrivateLink or the cloud's equivalent), with client IAM and client-owned logs. This is the closest practical equivalent to the course's VPC option, but the model runs in the cloud vendor's managed environment, not on infrastructure the client controls; say so in the security review and let the client's security team decide whether it meets their "VPC" requirement. For a true air-gap requirement, offer open-weight models on client GPUs or decline, and say so in discovery. Verify live, before each pitch, whether Claude is offered on-prem. Source: topic 36 real world. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit G1, G27, A4(R44 part): Bedrock/PrivateLink wording no longer claims client-controlled infrastructure)*

**R45.** Agree the eval contract in weeks 1–2: the customer's real questions, a written grading rule and a numeric threshold (e.g. 85 percent). Never negotiate the threshold the day before launch. Build the golden set failure-modes-first: list what would be unacceptable for this customer, turn each into an observable check, then add their real questions (R296). Source: topic 25 L2 (image). (old 4) *(changed 2026-11-01: adds failure-modes-first golden set)*

**R46.** Write the exit criteria in week one, and ask "who will own this?" in month one; both change how you build. Source: topic 41 L1.

## 2 · Prototype / PoC

**R47.** Give the PoC one job: prove the risky part (retrieval plus grounded, cited answers) on the customer's real data. Name the risky part for the project type: SQL correct on the client's real schema, extraction field accuracy on real invoices, tool-call correctness on the real API, and retrieval plus citation only for document Q&A. Source: topic 38 L1. (old 3) *(changed 2026-11-01 audit I-11: risky part named per project type)*

**R48.** Never swap in public, cleaned-up or rewritten documents, and never count a prototype that ran on your own sample documents. Source: topic 38 Q1; 02 L2; 39 Q3. (old 3)

**R49.** Build only the core, in order: customer docs → embed and store → retrieve → cited answer. Run it from a terminal or a plain script. The PoC must also be able to say "I do not have that in the documents": set a similarity floor tuned on the customer's own documents (R151) and test it with one out-of-scope question, because the deliberate-limit demo (R238) depends on it. Source: topic 38 L1 (image). *(changed 2026-11-01 audit H-08: PoC needs an abstain path)*

**R50.** Defer the web UI, auth/user accounts and the deployment pipeline to "Later". Cut everything around the core, but never retrieval or citations. Source: topic 38 L1 (image), Q1.

**R51.** Deliver in days, not weeks; aim for a demo by about day four. Put a working version in front of real users in days, not months. Source: topic 38 L1; 01 L1. (old 3)

**R52.** Never spend scarce PoC days on polish: 3 of 5 days on UI leaves 2 for the core. Source: topic 38 Q3.

**R53.** Treat crude fixed chunking (e.g. 500 characters) as a middle case: sometimes acceptable in a PoC, but it degrades the answer quality being judged. Source: topic 38 Q1.

**R54.** Find out in week one whether retrieval fails on messy PDFs, while scope can still change. Source: topic 38 L1.

**R55.** Make every PoC answer name its source document. Source: topic 38 T1.

**R56.** If real access is pending, build against a local mock with the same response shape so the demo date holds. Swap in the real endpoint when the scoped account arrives. Source: topic 23 Q1, T1.

**R57.** Exit the prototype only when it has run on real customer documents and one real user has watched it answer and said "useful". Source: topic 02 L2.

**R58.** Never roll a prototype out to all users. Say yes to the goal and no to the timeline, and propose a named hardening plan plus a small logged pilot (course: a two-week hardening plan and a 10-agent pilot). Source: topic 38 Q2. (old 31)

**R59.** [Hadavin practice] Build the PoC as a small typed package with pytest on the retrieval/citation core, a read-only connector, and regex PII redaction on from day one. Exit = a recorded terminal demo. Source: topic 38 real world.

## 3 · Build

### Calling models and prompts

**R60.** Send each call to the base URL + `/v1/chat/completions` with Bearer-auth and JSON headers, and a body of `model` plus `messages` (system, user and assistant roles). Source: topic 06 L1 (image).

**R61.** [Hadavin practice] Hide provider shapes behind one adapter, e.g. `chat(messages, system, model_tier) -> Reply`. Claude takes `system` as a top-level field and reports `stop_reason`. The adapter maps every provider stop reason to its own enum (done, truncated, tool_call, blocked, other), treats an unknown value as an error, and returns one normalized `Usage` (R362). The model is a kind: Claude default plus at least one alternative backend (R16, R363); vendor SDK imports stay inside the backend module. On Bedrock the Claude 5.x models are served through the Messages-API endpoint (Anthropic SDK Bedrock-Mantle client, ids like `anthropic.claude-sonnet-5-5`); Converse and InvokeModel are not listed on the 5.5 pages (the docs document them under "Opus 4.6 and earlier"); whether a 5.5 id works through Converse is [unverified 2026-11-01], so test the pinned id first. The Bedrock backend therefore has two code paths: `anthropic_messages` (transport variants: direct, bedrock-mantle, Foundry, Google Cloud) and `bedrock_converse` (other vendors' Bedrock models and Claude 4.6 and earlier); a second backend counts as an alternative only after the golden set passes on it (R16). Message Batches is not supported on the Bedrock Messages endpoint, so the 50 percent batch discount applies to the Claude API only: never quote batch savings to a Bedrock client (Bedrock's own batch product with 5.5 ids: [unverified 2026-11-01]) [research 2026-11-01: platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-amazon-bedrock, "API endpoints (Message Batches, Models, Admin, Compliance, Usage and Cost)" listed as unsupported; platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, "50 percent discount"]. Source: topic 06 real world. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A1, A2, B1, B9, M1: adapter contract, stop-reason enum, Bedrock Messages-API endpoint fact)* *(changed 2026-11-01 round 2: Bedrock two code paths; batch discount note)*

**R62.** Use `max_tokens` as a seatbelt. Course lab (notes line 1424): on its reasoning model leave it out or set it in the thousands, never `max_tokens: 1`, and check `finish_reason` (`length`) before trusting empty content. [Hadavin practice, Claude] The Claude Messages API requires `max_tokens` on every call, so the adapter always sets it per workload from config, sized for thinking plus answer (thinking tokens count against it), and maps a max-tokens stop reason to a truncation error, never to a normal empty answer. Reasoning depth on current Claude models is set by adaptive thinking plus an effort setting, never by a token budget (the old budget form is rejected with a 400 on Claude 4.7 and later, including the three 5.5 tiers; Claude 4.6 still accepts it, deprecated); pin effort per workload in config and record it with the model id (R290), because it changes cost, latency and cache hits. Among the 5.5 tiers thinking can be switched off only on Haiku 5.5 (effort high or below; Claude 4.6 allows `disabled`); on Sonnet 5.5 the lowest setting is `thinking: {type: "between_tools"}` (it rejects `disabled` with a 400); on Opus 5.5 lower the effort level [research 2026-11-01: platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking and /thinking-steering-and-cost, per-model table rows quoted in `..\egm\decisions-2026-11-01\rule-questions-and-vendor-facts.md` F3]. Source: topic 03 L1; 06 L1. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit A9 (disputed, see log), B2, B3: Claude requires max_tokens; adaptive thinking and effort; course text kept and labelled)* *(changed 2026-11-01 round 2: thinking-off per tier)*

**R63.** Put developer rules in one system message at the start of the list, never mixed into user text. Put the input in tags such as `<ticket>...</ticket>` and say that everything inside is data, never instructions (R196). It is strong guidance, not law, so back it with guardrails. Source: topic 06 L3 (image), Q1. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B12: input block marked as untrusted data)*

**R64.** Build prompts from a fixed template plus per-request data (f-strings); never type the finished prompt. Source: topic 05 L1, T1.

**R65.** Walk replies one step at a time: `reply["choices"][0]["message"]["content"]`, because `choices` is a list. Source: topic 05 L2, Q1.

**R66.** Stick to the cheap model while looping and learning: a loop makes spending money as easy as one keystroke. This is a learning-lab rule ("while learning", N:1556); a production workload's model is chosen by R278 to R281 and routed by R213. Source: topic 06 L2. *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read: scope)*

**R67.** Whatever the prompt leaves unsaid, the model decides for itself, and not the same way every run (course, N:2089); [Hadavin practice] so write an edge-case line and a golden item for every behaviour you care about. Write every prompt to the four-point checklist: the task in one sentence, the output shape, the must-include fields, and edge cases (empty, off-topic, missing field). Source: topic 08 L1.

**R68.** Make each prompt right the first time, every time: a pipeline has no next turn. Ten precise lines beat forty vague ones. Source: topic 08 L1.

**R69.** Switch to few-shot when you write "unless" for the third time: 2–4 solved examples covering the tricky cases, in identical output format, never 20. Source: topic 08 L2 (image), Q1.

**R70.** Control format with four techniques: state it as a rule, show a literal template, forbid extras (explanations, greetings, markdown), and combine with few-shot (the strongest). Source: topic 08 L3.

**R71.** Check output with the parser that consumes it, not by eye. Prompting gets the format right about 95% of the time, so plan validation and retries. Source: topic 08 L3.

**R72.** Give every production system prompt five sections: identity, scope, behavior, refusals and escalation, output rules. "You are a helpful assistant." is not one. Source: topic 09 L1 (image). (old 26)

**R73.** Get the policy from the customer's support lead, legal and brand teams: what the bot may promise, when it hands off, and which topics are off limits. Turn it into precise prompt text. Source: topic 09 L1. (old 26)

**R74.** Ship every prompt through draft → customer review → test against nasty inputs (off-topic, angry refund) → ship. Source: topic 09 L1, T1. (old 26)

**R75.** Keep each prompt in its own file under git, read by code. Never keep it as a code string edited over SSH. Source: topic 09 L2, Q2. (old 26)

**R76.** Make one prompt change per commit, with a message that says why and who asked, and test it on the nasty inputs before merge. Source: topic 09 L2, T2. (old 26)

**R77.** Pin the exact versioned model name, never a moving alias: you decide when to upgrade, not the provider. Source: topic 09 L3. (old 27)

**R78.** Keep 20–50 real inputs with known good outputs in the prompt repo. On a new model version, run them, diff the outputs, fix the prompts, then switch production. Diff format habits (extra lines, markdown), tone, and refusal or edge-case rate, not only correctness ("the refusal rule that worked at 99% now works at 92%", N:2772-2775). [Hadavin practice] Every PR runs the set against a recorded or fake backend (offline, free). A live run happens only on a change to a prompt file or a model id, in the client's own account with their credentials and a per-run spend cap from config; a live run in a studio-paid account needs the owner's yes with numbers. The set lives in the client's repo, redacted (R228), never in the studio root (R360). Source: topic 09 L3 (image). (old 27) *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B4: regression CI made compatible with no-spend and client-data rules)*

**R79.** On a deprecation notice, keep the pilot on the pinned version and test the new one now. Fix drift and schedule the switch before retirement. Don't change providers for a routine deprecation, but keep a tested alternative backend ready (R16). Record each pinned model id with the provider's published retirement date in the runbook, and subscribe the client owner and the studio to the provider's deprecation notices. Source: topic 09 Q1. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B1, B13: alternative backend ready; retirement dates recorded)*

**R80.** [Hadavin practice] Back the prompt's escalation text with a real handoff action (a ticket or human queue) and an audit log. Source: topic 09 real world.

### Structured output

**R81.** Ask for data, not prose. Show the exact JSON schema with every key, the allowed values and the null case decided in advance. Source: topic 10 L1, L2 (images). (old 28)

**R82.** Say "Output only the JSON object. No explanation before or after it. No markdown, no code fences." Then strip fences and slice from the first `{` to the last `}` in code anyway. Source: topic 10 L2, T3. (old 28)

**R83.** Keep the temperature low or at default for extraction. With thinking on, check live which sampling parameters the model accepts; do not assume temperature can be set. Source: topic 10 L2. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B3: thinking mode caveat)*

**R84.** Validate every parsed record in plain code or a typed schema: first check that the parsed value is an object (a list, string, number or null is a validation error, not an exception), then required keys, allowed values (every enum field) and types. At 99% success, 1,000 tickets a day still means ~10 broken records. Source: topic 10 L3. (old 28) *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B5: object check first, every enum checked)*

**R85.** On a parse or validation failure, retry once with the named error ("Your previous reply was invalid: <error>. Reply again with only the corrected JSON object."). Cap it at 2 attempts; the cap counts validation or parse attempts only, and transport retries (R351) have their own budget. Feed the retry a sanitized error (field path and error type), never the offending value or raw library text, which can carry untrusted or personal text back into the call and the trace. Source: topic 10 L3 (image). (old 28) *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B6, B14: separate retry budgets; sanitized retry error)*

**R86.** Write inputs that fail twice to a failures file for a human. Never loop forever and never skip silently. Source: topic 10 L3, Q1. (old 28)

**R87.** [Hadavin practice] Use the platform's schema or forced-tool output and still validate in code. A record that fails twice never writes to the ERP; it waits for human approval. Source: topic 10 real world.

### Tools

**R88.** Treat a tool request as data, not action: nothing runs until your code decides to execute it. Source: topic 11 L1 (image), Q1.

**R89.** Define each tool with a name, a description of what it does and when to use it, and JSON-Schema parameters with types, a required list and example values ("City name, for example Rotterdam"). Source: topic 11 L2.

**R90.** Keep tools small and single-purpose; never build one giant tool with a mode argument. Optional parameters stay out of `required`. Source: topic 11 L2, T3.

**R91.** Run the tool loop: check the reply kind, `json.loads` the arguments, run the function, then append the assistant request followed by a tool message with the matching `tool_call_id`. Detect a tool request by the stop reason or the presence of tool-call blocks, never by empty text (Claude may return text beside a tool call). Loop over every tool call in the reply and answer each id in order; treat unparseable arguments as a tool error result (R105), never a crash; keep sending the tool definitions on the next call and cap the iterations (R102). Source: topic 11 L3 (image). *(changed 2026-11-01 audit C-02, C-03, C-15: tool-request detection, parallel calls, bad arguments)*

**R92.** Make tool descriptions say what each tool returns, including ids the next tool needs. If a 3-step human task takes the agent 8 steps, fix the descriptions first. Source: topic 21 L1.

**R93.** [Hadavin practice] Validate tool arguments against the schema in code before execution, and treat them as untrusted input (R349). The model may also fill optional parameters the user never gave (the course's routing task produced `priority: medium`); for state-changing tools confirm any value not present in the user's words, and let the tool-routing eval check for invented optional arguments. Log every tool call (name, argument hash, result size, latency) in the R221 line, and use the tool-choice setting (auto, required, forced) in routing evals. Source: topic 11, 43 real world. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit C-17, C-18, E3: invented optional arguments; pointer to R349)*

### Conversation state

**R94.** The model remembers nothing. Keep one messages list per conversation, append every user **and** assistant turn, and send the whole list on each call. Source: topic 12 L1, L2 (images), Q1. (old 29)

**R95.** Keep the system message on top, with user and assistant turns alternating in order; shuffling them changes the dialogue. Source: topic 12 L2.

**R96.** Cap long histories. Trim to the system message plus the last N turns (`MAX_TURNS = 8`) when old turns stop mattering, and summarize when early facts matter; production often does both. Source: topic 12 L3 (image). (old 29)

**R171.** R171 is a cheap fail-closed gate, never the only control: a money amount must also be compared as a TYPED FIELD (integer minor units) and approved by a person; free-text number matching is evidence, not proof. [Hadavin practice] Match numbers by value, never by substring or a bare boundary regex (so "1,500" is read as the number fifteen hundred and never counts as 500). (1) What is read: a run of ASCII digits 0-9 joined only by the separators the client's locale config names, read as a `Decimal`. A run that is not exactly one number in that locale gives no number ("1 500" where a space does not group thousands, "7,5" where the comma groups thousands, "3,4"), and so does any run with a leading zero ("0040", "000", "00.5", "0,500"; "0.5" is read). In front of the run there may be ONE currency symbol or upper-case currency code from config, directly or after one ASCII space, and in front of that a plain minus sign ("-" or U+2212), which makes the value negative ("-€40", "-EUR40", "EUR -40", "about -40" read -40); two currency tokens in front ("EUR €40", "EUR -€40") give no number. (2) Left neighbour of that token: the start of the text, a newline, or exactly one ASCII space whose own left neighbour is a letter, or one of . , ; : ! ? = that does not follow a digit. (3) Right neighbour: at most ONE currency token for the whole number (a glued symbol or code, or one space and a symbol, code or currency name from config such as euro, euros, dollar, dollars), and then ONLY: the end; a newline; one of . , ; ! ? followed by the end, a newline, or one space and a SENTENCE-START word; or ")" when no "(" comes before the token and the ")" is followed by the end, a newline or that punctuation rule. After a newline, blank lines are skipped and the next non-empty line must begin with a SENTENCE-START word. A SENTENCE-START word begins with an ASCII capital A-Z (any other first character, including non-Latin scripts, lower case, digits, "%" and fraction characters, gives no number) and is not in the UNION DENY SET: range and joining words (and, or, to, plus, with, from, between, minus, less, more, over, under, bis, und, oder, à, et, ou, y, o and the like), qualifiers (approximately, about, around and the like), every number word of the configured languages (see (7)), fraction and quantity words (half, quarter, third, double, triple, twice, couple, pair, dozen, score, once, fortnight) and scale and unit words (million, Million, thousand, lakh, crore, k, bn, cents, pence, percent, per, mille, Millionen, Tausend, duizend, miljoen, milione, milhão, bin, milyon and the like). So any word directly after the number or its currency token, any lower-case word after ". , ; ! ?", and any denied word after them give no number: "EUR 40, thousand", "EUR 40. Million", "EUR 40" with a next line starting "And 50 cents" or "Half", and the scale words in every listed language give no number. (4) Also no number when a dash-like character (Unicode category Pd, or U+2212, U+207B, U+208B, U+02D7, U+2796) sits in the 8 characters before the token or the 3 after the run; when the word right before it (after one space, ": " or a newline) is "minus" or "credit"; or under the RANGE-SENTENCE rule: earlier in the same sentence (since the last . ! ? followed by whitespace, or the last newline; at most 300 characters back) there is a range connector (from, between, to, till, until, through, and, or, plus, with; von, zwischen, bis, und, oder; de, entre, à, au, et, ou; desde, hasta, y, o; e, tot, en, tussen, van, da, fino; configured for en, de, fr, es, nl, it, pt) AND a digit run or a number word: "ranging from EUR 40 per day to EUR 50.", "vierzig bis 50 EUR" and "quarante à 50 euros" give no 50. Any sentence with two numbers and a joining word therefore fails closed, which is an accepted cost. A CRLF line ending is read as a newline. (5) Not read, named limits: meaning words on the LEFT of a number (not, no, never, over, under, at least, at most, more than, less than, instead of, except, approx., No., Mr., section, page, order, ticket, id, debit, loss, shortfall, refund, owed, negative, less, "credit of", was/now pairs): "The limit is EUR 40, not EUR 400." reads 400, so every positive check must be paired with `must_not_contain` and the judge. The currency identity is not compared ("USD 40." passes an expected 40 for a EUR item: compare the currency as a typed field). In a locale that groups thousands with a space (fr), digit groups in 3-digit steps joined by a space read as one number ("voir 7 100." reads 7100: accepted false pass), so the locale config must match the document language (en "40.000" is 40, de "40.000" is 40000). An unlisted ASCII capitalised scale word after ". " or on the next line is a residual risk ("EUR 40. Mds" reads 40). The first word after . ! ? or on the next line is only checked to be an ASCII capital outside the deny set; its meaning is not judged: "EUR 40. ## 9 · Model decisions (ML-engineer hat)

**R277.** Never pick a model on quality alone: weigh quality, latency and cost per use case. Source: topic 07 L1 (image).

**R278.** Profile each workload: how hard is one call, is a human waiting, how many calls a day. Not hard → small fast model; hard and low volume → big model; hard and high volume → test the small one first. A human waiting points to the small fast model; a hard task with a human waiting needs a measured p95 latency budget before the big model is chosen. The course also shows a fourth axis, in its examples rather than as a fourth question: what a wrong answer costs. A legal summary a lawyer will sign counts as hard (N:1954); the contracts workload goes to the big model because "accuracy matters far more than speed or cost" (N:1991, N:2027); forcing the cheap model everywhere "risks quality where it actually matters" (N:2049). [Hadavin practice, stricter than the course chart, which says hard and high volume → test the small one first] Write the cost of a wrong answer in the profile (money moved, a signature, a regulated decision, a customer told something false); a high cost moves the workload to the strong model, or to a person, even at high volume, unless the same-20 evidence (R279) shows the cheap model agrees on every money field. Source: topic 07 L2 (image). *(changed 2026-11-01 audit B7: waiting human implication)* *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read A5: cost of a wrong answer)*

**R279.** Start cheap where the chart says so, and upgrade only with evidence. Easy work, high-volume work and work where a person waits start on the cheap model (N:1958); hard and high-volume work tests the cheap model first ("test flash first", image 175955). Run 20 real examples on the cheap model (N:1975) ([Hadavin practice] in a sales demo the 20 are made-up examples; in a pilot they are the client's own samples, owner decision 10); if they are good enough, stop. If not, run the next tier up on the same 20 and show the customer the difference, "not a feeling" (N:1976). Run both tiers on the 20 also when the customer needs agreement shown (N:2037-2042), and never a whole batch through both (R283). Hard and low-volume work goes to the big model on the course chart (image 175955; the contracts workload, N:1991, N:2011, N:2027); a cheap-model test there is optional and worth it only when cost matters. This is a design-time step, done once per workload before launch and again on a model change (topic 09 L3); it is not run-time behaviour. When a customer demands the biggest model everywhere, split by workload and show agreement on 20 of their own examples, "evidence instead of opinion" (N:2042): do not simply obey (it "wastes money for identical results", N:2049) and do not answer with a talk about the triangle (Q1 option 4, N:2039, is not the best move). Source: topic 07 L2 (image), T2, Q1. (old 20) *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read A6, A8)* *(changed 2026-11-04 pass 2: the chart sends hard, low-volume work to the big model)*

**R280.** Start cheap because "downgrade later" rarely happens: once the expensive model is live, nobody touches it (N:1977). "Start cheap" is about choosing a workload's model with evidence before launch; it does not mean sending each request to the cheap model first and retrying on a stronger one (no run-time cascade appears in topics 07 or 33; see R213, R290). For hard, low-volume work the chart starts on the big model (see R279). Source: topic 07 L2. *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read: design time, no cascade)*

**R281.** Mix models per workload in one project, e.g. the classifier on the cheap model and the report writer on the big one. Source: topic 07 L2, Q1.

**R282.** Measure latency, tokens and cost per call yourself; single-call timings vary. The course's own race shows pro faster (1.174 s) than flash (1.825 s) while its text says flash "came back faster" (N:1910, N:1916, N:1950). [Hadavin practice] Quote latency or cost per model only from at least 20 measured calls. Source: topic 07 L1, T1. *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read A11)*

**R283.** Never run a whole batch through both models "to compare": that spends the money you meant to save. Source: topic 07 Q2.

**R284.** Send missing knowledge to RAG and behavior to prompting. Fine-tuning learns skills and habits, not reliable facts. Source: topic 30 L1, L2 (image). (old 19)

**R285.** Climb the cost ladder only on evidence: prompting (minutes), then RAG (days), then fine-tuning (weeks, plus a model to maintain forever). Source: topic 30 L2.

**R286.** Fine-tune only when all three hold: it's a behavior problem; eval scores prove prompting is at its limit; volume or consistency justifies weeks of work. Source: topic 30 L2 (image). (old 19)

**R287.** Treat an approved fine-tuning budget as pressure, not an argument. Manuals updated monthly go to RAG. Source: topic 30 Q1.

**R288.** Split mixed requests: changing facts (e.g. a catalog every two weeks) go to RAG, and an eval-proven format gap goes to fine-tuning. Source: topic 30 Q3.

**R289.** Fine-tune the cheap model when a behavior task has plateaued below target and labels exist (course: 42 categories, 81% vs 90%, 30,000 labels, 50,000 emails/month), and the volume or consistency need justifies it (R286). For a pure classification task with thousands of labels, run the classifier baseline first (R364). Count the saved prompt tokens in the case ("prompt compression": behaviour forced by a 3000-token system prompt gets baked in, N:11616), and [Hadavin practice] cost the bigger-model experiment at real volume before quoting a fine-tune (it "scales the cost of every one of 50,000 monthly calls", N:11720). Source: topic 30 Q2. *(changed 2026-11-04 course re-read A24)* *(changed 2026-11-01 audit F8, F18: volume condition restored; classifier baseline pointer)*

**R290.** [Hadavin practice] Escalate tiers one at a time (Haiku 5.5 → Sonnet 5.5 → Opus 5.5) with the eval result in hand, and record each choice for the client. Each tier choice is a design-time decision from eval evidence (R279, R280); at run time a workload uses its one chosen tier, or sits behind a classify-first router (R213). ### Prove it (additions)

**R322.** [Hadavin practice] For any agent with tools, report pass^k beside the pass rate: run each golden item k times (k of 3 to 5, set by cost) and report the share that passes all k; [at hardening] gate on both. With a true per-run pass rate p and independent runs, pass^k = p^k: at p = 0.90 it is 0.729 for k = 3 and 0.590 for k = 5, so a "90%" agent is not a reliable one. Real tasks are not independent, so measure it, don't compute it (the book's own example of a fall from 61% to 25% over 8 tries cannot come from independent runs, since 0.61 to the 8th is about 0.02, so do not reuse it). **R323.** [Hadavin practice] For any system that approves, denies or flags, report the two error types separately (false approve versus false deny; precision versus recall of flags). **R325.** [Hadavin practice, at hardening] Calibrate every LLM judge before it gates anything. A judge is a pass/fail classifier validated against an expert's labels: hand-label at least 100 items (150 when a miss costs money or security, R175), split into a dev part of at least 50 for tuning the rubric and a held-out test part of at least 50 (at least 100 for the high-stakes tier) that no one tunes on. The test part holds at least 25 human-pass and at least 25 human-fail items (at least 40 each for the high-stakes tier), oversampling failures on purpose, and includes the R356 injection case, the R357 confident invention and, while the judge is same-family (R175), at least 20 human-failed assistant outputs. Read it once and report: true positive rate and true negative rate with exact 95% intervals, percent agreement, Cohen's kappa with its interval and the confusion matrix (kappa is reported, not gated). The judge may gate only if the one-sided 95% lower bound of BOTH rates is at least 0.70 (at least 22 of 25 in each class) and both audit cases are caught; the high-stakes tier needs 0.80 on the true negative rate (37 of 40) and 0.75 on the true positive rate (35 of 40). Raw agreement is never reported alone. If it fails, tune on the dev part only and label a fresh test part; after two failed test parts stop, do not gate on this judge and escalate to the owner. Fewer than 100 labels is a smoke calibration. Re-calibrate on any change of model or rubric and run the judge on a sample of live traffic. A judge calibrated on 100 labels has its own error of about 10 to 15%, so a contract number on the locked part is read from human-verified verdicts when that part has 150 items or fewer; a judge-only locked score is allowed only with a cross-family judge (unreachable until a second backend exists, R175) and reports a bias-corrected interval that includes the calibration uncertainty. Extends R176. *(changed 2026-11-01 audit J-01: floor raised to 100 labels with at least 50 held out)* *(changed 2026-11-01 round 2: per-class lower bounds replace the kappa gate; retry limit; high-stakes tier; supersedes audit J-01 wording and the ROLES kappa floor; needs the owner's yes)* *(round 3: judge-only locked score only with a cross-family judge)*

**R326.** [Hadavin practice, at hardening] Report an exact (Clopper-Pearson, two-sided 95%) interval beside every gated score, and size the gating set from it. Computed for this book (python, bisection on the binomial tail, 2026-11-01, rounded outward): 17 of 20 passes (85%) gives 62.1% to 96.8%, 43 of 50 (86%) gives 73.2% to 94.2%, 170 of 200 (85%) gives 79.2% to 89.7%; even 20 of 20 only supports a true pass rate of at least 83.1%, and 50 of 50 at least 92.8%. One flipped item moves a 20-item score 5 points. So 10 to 20 items suit a discovery sketch or PoC, and a gate tied to a contract number needs 50 or more, bucketed (R165), with more where one wrong answer is costly. A one-sided upper bound is the right form for a ceiling metric ("the failure rate may not exceed X"), and a set under 50 items is a smoke result and is labelled "smoke, n = ..." in every report. A verdict word needs enough answered items. The one-sided 95% upper bound of the error rate is at most 10% only after at least 29 answered items with none wrong, 46 with one, 61 with two, 76 with three. The intervals above are two-sided and describe a score. The verdict is a separate, one-sided test: print the one-sided upper bound beside the verdict word and say "one-sided" (1 wrong of 50 is 0.091 one-sided, green; its two-sided upper bound 0.106 is not the green test). Answered means the system gave a final answer to an answerable item; hand-offs and "Not sure" are not answered and count as misses in the contract count (R191). Traps and no-answer items are added on top, because they are not answerable and R165 needs traps at 15% or more of the whole set: for example 60 answerable items at a 0.9 answer rate plus 11 traps is a set of 71 (11 of 71 is 15.5%; 10 of 70 would be 14.3%). Under 50 answered the report says "smoke, n = ..." with the bound and no verdict word. For a client expecting a green, plan about 100 answered when the true error is expected near 3% (82% chance of green) and about 180 near 5% (80%).
