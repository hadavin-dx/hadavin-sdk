## 1. The question, in plain words
A value V that the redactor already knows is replaced by a placeholder when written plainly. The same V written with look-alike letters, other case, one combining mark, zero-width characters inside, full-width forms, or split over two lines goes to the model unchanged. What is the smallest design that makes such a copy impossible to send?
**One answer.** Any copy that a fold of the text still reads as V makes the redactor REFUSE the whole text (the check, section 5). Draft 5's second answer, a case pass that masked other-case copies of stored names and addresses in place, is DELETED in draft 6 (round 5, ADV-LK5-2, -4, -10; order 8).
**What "a folded copy" means in this note (exactly):** a text one of whose scan keys contains a scan key of V. The scan key is `scan_keyset` of P1b (NFKD and the confusables table, case folded in two readings, class maps `z` to `3` and, from P1 revision 11, `l` to `i`, every character that is not a letter or digit removed (revision 11; revision 10 removed whitespace only), tag texts T0-T3). The text is read as written AND after `runmap.undo` (JSON, backslash, percent and entity layers decoded). Anything a reader can see as V only through a transformation outside these two functions is NOT covered (section 10).
**Scope, stated plainly.** (a) This closes the table for the core repository (`hadavin_sdk`). Warden's own `redaction.redact` is not fixed in the Warden repository (lead order 13(2)): when Warden is promoted, its redaction module is REPLACED by the core one (the retire list), so the fix for Warden is this check; section 8 gives the retire-list line and the Warden handover ticket text. (b) Stored NAME and ADDRESS values follow the evidence rule of lead order 13(1) (section 7, precondition P-1): if benign-document refusals with NAME and ADDRESS checked are at or below 1 in 200 texts, they are IN the v1 check; if above, they stay out, and the gap is one CHANGELOG line plus a row in the lead's later-fixes list. Between those two, this draft adds one measured middle step (checked only on texts holding a non-ASCII character that the scan key can read as a letter or digit; PL-LK-32). Until P-1 runs, which of the three P2d-2 builds is NOT decided; the code is built after P-1, for the outcome P-1 gives. Every other known value (secrets and identifiers, with a raw length of 12 or more) is covered on every path in the table.

## 3. Three designs compared
**(a) Span-mask.** Fold, find V's key, map the hit back to written positions, replace only that span. Needs a position map; `_aligned_fold` (redact.py 1082) gives one only for simple folds and returns None otherwise (COR-R4-4), and then the line is withheld anyway. P2d-1's alias index is a smaller version of this idea and drew findings in rounds 3 to 5 (ADV-R3-2, ADV-R3-3, ADV-R3-8, ADV-R5-5, ADV-R5-6). Best fidelity, largest surface. Rejected for v1 (v1.1 LKV-1).
**(b) Refuse the whole text.** After all masking, if a scan key of V occurs in a scan key of the text, refuse. No position map. A person looks.
**(c) The round-4 fallback (`deny_hit` over the final output).** Gone: `deny_hit`, `prepare_needles` and `id_keyset` are cut from P1 v1 (P1 rev 10, PENDING LEAD 27). Its useful part, the scan key, is `scan_keyset`, which (b) calls directly. GOLD-1 ties the check to a plain-`in` oracle over the same keys (section 6).
**Pick: (b), and nothing else.** Why: every table row is closed by an `in`-style check on folded keys; the failure is a refusal, not a leak (fail closed); the span machinery that kept failing review is not needed. What it costs: the whole text is lost when it holds a variant of a known value, including an upper-case copy of a known secret. **On the agent-loop paths a refusal ends the run**: the document path reports `redaction_error` (agent_loop 376-377; only `InputTooLarge` is reported as `document_too_large`, 371-375), tool results 469-470, tool inputs 495-496, the proposal check 649-650, all through `_redaction_failed` (547-549: a `guard/redaction failed` note and an abstention). **On the degrade paths it becomes a fixed text**: `audit/capped.py` 72-73 (withheld marker), `tools.error_reason` and `safe_head` (`SAFE_HEAD_NOTE`), the case bundle (`MASKED` per string, new in draft 6), and the approval request summary (its existing fallback card, section 5).
Size, honestly (an ESTIMATE): about 15 lines refactored in `_build` (one shared grouping helper for the sweep finder and the look-alike finder), about 60 new lines in `redact.py` and `runmap.py` (one key type, the check with its decoded form, the joined `redact_many` check, the extra originals, the exception), one keyword parameter through `redact_text`, `redact_bounded`, `redact_untrusted`, `redact_layered` and `prepare_output`, about 5 lines in `error_reason`, one exception name in `k1b/redaction.py`, one changed string in `approvals/requests.py`, 2 or 3 constants, and only for the middle outcome of P-1 about 6 lines of gate. CUT in draft 6: the case pass (about 20 lines, a flags change of `_compile_uncached`, a match-to-original rule), the 8-line approval-card handler (round-5 resolve).

## 4. Why a plain fold is not enough (decides the dependency)
My draft-1 prototype used the snapshot `fold` with case folding and all whitespace removed. All table rows were caught, but the table reads some look-alikes as a different character: Cyrillic Ze (U+0437) reads `3`, so a V holding `z` written with Ze is missed; Cyrillic Be (U+0431) reads `6`; small Greek eta (U+03B7) reads `n`. Confirmed in `_confusables.py`. P1b's class map (`z` to `3`) and two case readings close Ze and capital Eta. **So the check needs `scan_keyset`, and P2d-2 FOLLOWS P1b** (order 11; P1 rev 10 "What downstream packages wait for"). P1 keeps no class map for digit-shaped letters (PENDING LEAD 29, narrowed by PENDING LEAD 36 to digits): a known value that holds the DIGIT and is written with such a source is found; one that holds the LETTER and is written with a digit-shaped source (Be for `b`) is a leetspeak rewrite, not a look-alike, and stays open (LKV-3, xfail LK-BE). Class maps live in P1b only; this design adds none.
**What P1 revision 11 changes here (P1 round 9 resolve, PENDING LEAD 35 to 37; revision 11 is being written, so every row below that depends on it says so):** the keys keep letters and digits only and map `l` to `i`. So dots, hyphens, underscores, `?` and U+FFFD between the letters of V are removed from the key, and capital I written for small l reads the same: those copies are REFUSED (rows LK-FILL, LK-IL, LK-SURR), and the key-length rule of section 5 counts after that removal. If revision 11 does not take PENDING LEAD 36, LK-FILL (except U+FFFD, dropped by SK-6 already), LK-IL and LK-SURR return to the strict xfail list. `has_hidden_channel` keeps rules 1 to 4 (PENDING LEAD 35); this design never relied on rules 5 to 7.
P1 supplies no percent or JSON decoder (its entity step decodes HTML entities only). The decoded form therefore comes from this design, through the existing `runmap.undo` (runmap.py 139-170), not from P1.
The design uses no other P1 function: `squeeze` and `fold` are not called; P1 keeps both public for their own callers (PENDING LEAD 37).

## 5. The design (b), exactly
**New public function** `check_lookalike(texts, mapping, *, extra=(), _cache=None, _deadline=None) -> None` in `guard/redact.py`; it raises **`LookalikeCopy`**, a new public subclass of `runmap.RedactionError` defined in `runmap.py` and raised from `redact.py` through a function-level import (the precedent: `_Redactor._over_budget`, redact.py 986-992, raises `InputTooLarge` the same way; `runmap.undo` is imported the same way). Message = class name only. Bare callers that catch `RedactionError` catch it. **Every textnorm call of the check (`scan_keyset` on each original, on each text and on its decoded form) is made in the body of `check_lookalike` itself, and its only exception handler, which names `TextnormError` and counts a hit, is there too**: `check_lookalike` is the name listed in P1's `REFUSAL_HANDLERS` (P1 rev 10, "the caller rule and the refusal lint" (b)); no helper and no memo function calls textnorm (ADV-LK5-8). The private method `_Redactor.lookalike_hit(keys)` receives keys that are already built and only searches (no textnorm call, no handler). There is no `except Exception` around a textnorm call (lint (a); COR-R8-9: the finder's own fallback handler wraps no textnorm call).
1. **Values and keys.** Every original of `mapping` except `"\0seed"` entries (round-5 resolve: kept; FR-13 now has an input that kills its removal) and except, in two of the three P-1 outcomes, originals whose kind is in `defaults.LOOKALIKE_WORD_KINDS = ("NAME", "ADDRESS")` (below), plus every string in `extra` (treated as a secret; never added to the map). There is no list of included kinds: a kind added to the redactor later is checked by default (ADV-LK4-9). **One length rule for every kind:** the RAW original has at least `defaults.LOOKALIKE_MIN_RAW_CHARS = 12` characters (`len(original)`, spaces counted) AND each key of `scan_keyset(original)` has at least `defaults.LOOKALIKE_MIN_KEY_CHARS = 8` characters, counted after the key's removals; a key under 8 is dropped. So a secret of 12 or more characters, a 36-character UUID, a 14-character id written with spaces are covered; an 11-character SSN-shaped value, a 10-character PAN, any secret of 8 to 11 characters, and a 12-character secret of which 5 or more characters are not letters or digits are not (xfail LK-SHORT; FR-6b; LKV-2; said in the owner summary, ADV-LK5-13). Keys are built once per original and kept in a per-run memo `cache["lookalike_keys"]` (original -> its keys, or the marker "unfoldable"; in the run's cache, never module-level, R371). **An original that `scan_keyset` refuses (`TextnormError`) counts as a hit: the check raises `LookalikeCopy` for every checked text while that value is in the map** (fail closed; P1 rev 10 error contract, ADV-R8-5, PENDING LEAD 34; row LK-UNFOLD). Cost: one unfoldable stored value ends the run at the next check (stated, section 10).
   **Word kinds (NAME, ADDRESS): built for the outcome of P-1 (section 7; lead order 13(1)).** Outcome IN: no exclusion, `LOOKALIKE_WORD_KINDS` is not added, word kinds are checked like every other kind. Outcome IN-GATED (the middle step, PL-LK-32): word-kind keys are searched in a checked form (the text, or its decoded form) only when that form holds at least one GATE character: a character outside ASCII that is not whitespace and not in the private set `_GATE_EXEMPT` of 16 typographic characters (U+2018, U+2019, U+201C, U+201D, U+2013, U+2014, U+2026, U+2022, U+00B7, U+20AC, U+00A3, U+20B9, U+00B0, U+00A9, U+00AE, U+00A7); none of the 16 has an ASCII letter or digit in its NFKD form or its confusables value (round-5 resolve fact 4, on the snapshot table), and row LK-GATE pins that from the table. Whole categories cannot be exempted: 41 punctuation and currency characters of the table read as ASCII letters (fact 4). Outcome OUT: word-kind originals are skipped, as in draft 5. Effect per outcome: IN closes every table row for names and addresses and refuses benign texts the way P-1 measured; IN-GATED refuses every look-alike, combining-mark, accented, full-width (bare path) and percent-encoded Cyrillic copy, but an all-capitals, lower-case or re-broken copy in a text with no gate character is SENT (row LK-WORD-ASCII); OUT sends every variant copy of a stored name or address (rows LK-WORD, LK-WORD-ASCII). **Nothing is masked in place any more for these kinds:** draft 5's case pass is deleted (it masked `BACKUP_DIR` as `<NAME_1>_DIR` after `username: backup` and restored `backup_DIR` into a tool call, ADV-LK5-2; and a `re.IGNORECASE` match cannot be mapped back by a case-folded lookup, ADV-LK5-4, fact 3).
2. **Finder.** The grouping code of `_build` (nesting measure, `_split`, `_slow`, the fallback to one-by-one) is factored into ONE helper used by the sweep finder and the look-alike finder. The look-alike finder is built from the KEYS (already case-folded by P1), so it is literal and case-sensitive like the sweep finder and `_compile_uncached` needs no flags parameter (COR-LK5-3 closed by the deletion of the case pass). **A match maps back by exact string:** the finder reports the matched key string; a dict built with the finder maps each key to the placeholder of the first original (longest first, then insertion order) that produced it, or to `<HANDED_SECRET>` for an `extra` value; two originals with an equal key share that label, which is used only for the log line (COR-LK5-4). The look-alike state is cached as `cache["lookalike"] = (map snapshot, extra, word-gate flag, finders, key-to-label dict, slow)` and reused only when the snapshot EQUALS the current map and `extra` is equal (never keyed by length: a clear-and-update to a different same-size map would reuse a stale finder, a leak). Finders are rebuilt when the map changes, the same rate as the sweep's own rebuild (redact.py 1310); thanks to the memo only new originals are folded. With a plain dict and no cache (bare callers), keys are rebuilt on every call (cost printed by FR-15; nothing survives the call, row LK-NOCACHE). In outcome IN-GATED the word-kind keys have a finder of their own, used only on a form that holds a gate character.
3. **Text, and its decoded form.** For each checked text t, the check reads t and, when different, `runmap.undo(t)` (JSON `\uXXXX` and one-character escapes, backslash escapes, percent-encoding, HTML entities, NFKC and invisible characters, to a fixed point; nested deeper than `defaults.SCRUB_MAX_LAYERS` it raises `TooDeep`, a `RedactionError`, which propagates: fail closed). In each, every genuine placeholder (a token matching `redact._PLACEHOLDER` AND a key of the map) is replaced by ONE ASCII space; any other `<UPPER_n>` token is ordinary text (today `sweep` skips every such token, so `<ABCD1234EFGH_5>`, an upper-case spelling of an alphanumeric value, survives in model-written text and on bare paths; on `redact_untrusted` such a token is first rewritten with `‹ ›` by `escape_placeholders`, runmap.py 320-325, whose angle quotes the scan key removes; either way the letters are read; row LK-FAKEPH, COR-LK5-11). The result goes through `scan_keyset` once; every key is searched with the finder. `scan_keyset` removes whitespace (and, from revision 11, every non-letter, non-digit), so **the pieces either side of a placeholder are joined on purpose**: a copy of V that the attacker splits by writing a maskable value (an e-mail, an IP) in its middle is read (row LK-PH). A space, not deletion, so that the pieces cannot form a new entity or combining sequence across the junction before the removals (row LK-PH-ENT). (No stand-in character: P1b drops U+FFFD, SK-6 and RES-1.) A hit returns the label of section 5 item 2 (for the log line; never the value). An empty key set returns at once (no `undo`, no key call).
4. **Fail closed and budget.** `TextTooLong`, `NotText` and any other `TextnormError`, from the text or from an original, count as a hit (`LookalikeCopy`). This includes a benign non-ASCII text that the redactor accepts but whose NFKD expansion is over `TEXTNORM_MAX_CHARS` (accents about x2, some Hangul about x3; P1 rev 10, the size rule): with a non-empty key set it is refused (row FR-33; P-1 counts it; COR-LK5-6, ADV-LK5-12). Every other exception propagates: `redact_text` turns a non-`RedactionError` into `RedactionError` (its existing handler around the `redact()` calls); a bare caller gets the exception and no text. `scan_keyset` has no clock (P1 rev 10 SK-4): the budget `deadline` (the caller's `_deadline`, or for a bare call one the check starts itself, `time.thread_time() + REDACT_BUDGET_S`) is checked before `undo` and before every `scan_keyset` call, and past it the check raises `InputTooLarge`. `REDACT_BUDGET_S = 6.0` is CPU seconds of the calling thread (`time.thread_time()`, runmap.py 484); the `defaults.py` comment that says "wall-clock" is wrong and P2d-2 corrects it. The longest uninterrupted step is one `scan_keyset` call (S_call, measured by the P1b evidence runner; about 2 s by multiplication, UNVERIFIED), or one `undo` of a text at the cap (UNVERIFIED). Added work per `redact_text`: at most 2 checked texts (`out` and `undo(out)`) x one `scan_keyset` call. The check shares the redactor's budget. Today a benign text at the cap already takes 2.6 s (many IP addresses) to 4.0 s (a zero-width-split `password=`) of the 6.0 s (defaults.py 34-37, measured on a laptop, 2026-11-02), and `channel.clean` makes two `redact_text` calls; whether the check fits is measured BEFORE P2d-2 is built (section 7, precondition P-2, with its rule written down now).

## 7. False-refusal rate and cost: what is and is not known, and two preconditions
Not measured on real documents; no figure is guessed. Known: (1) after masking, a PLAIN copy of V is normally a placeholder. The sweep leaves plain copies in three production cases (an identifier inside a longer ASCII letter/digit run, words in `_COMMON`, values under 3 characters or all-digit under 6); only the first produces refusals (`_COMMON` words are at most 10 characters and short values are under the minimum). (2) Chance matches are negligible for random secrets and ids: the draft-1 prototype found 0 hits for 8,000 random 12-40-character keys in 262,144 characters; by arithmetic, a random 8-character key matches about 262,144 / 36^8 = 9e-8 per text per value, a 12-digit number about 2.6e-7 (revision 11's `l` to `i` map makes the alphabet 35, a change below the precision of these figures). (3) Word kinds: their refusal cost depends on the P-1 outcome (FR-17, FR-24, FR-29, FR-31a). (4) The named risks that remain in every outcome: other-case copies of secrets and of case-sensitive identifiers (LK-UP, FR-30), identifiers glued to letters or digits (FR-11, FR-23), long quoted captures (FR-19), split copies around a placeholder (FR-21), Mn-only differences (FR-5), an unfoldable stored value (LK-UNFOLD), benign near-cap texts whose expansion is over the fold limit (FR-33), and the wider class rules of P1 revision 11 (every non-alphanumeric removed, `l` read as `i`).
**Precondition P-1 (false refusals; ADV-LK4-11; lead order 13(1)).** After P1b merges and before P2d-2 is built (the lead's `S0 EXIT` for P2d-2 waits for it), an evidence runner runs a throwaway prototype of the check on P1b's `scan_keyset` (not repository code) over a made-up corpus of **400 texts**: 200 invoices and letters (English ones with typographic quotes, dashes and currency signs; German and French ones with accented letters; some address blocks in capitals), 100 tool results (ERP/database style with padded columns, one with ANSI colour, one Word/PDF-style extract with private-use bullets, ADV-R9-5 of the P1 round 9), 100 model tool-call arguments; plus near-cap texts in Korean, Vietnamese and Arabic (ADV-LK5-12). Stored names, addresses, user names, tenant ids, cloud resource ids and passwords of 8 to 16 characters, some dictionary words, written plain, in capitals, padded, re-broken and accented. Output in `handover/evidence/lookalike-1.md`: the refusal rate per kind; the count of `TextTooLong` refusals of benign near-cap texts; the count of copies of stored names and addresses that a variant leaves unrefused (sent); and the word-kind measurements below. **Word-kind rule (lead order 13(1), written here and in P2d rev 6):** a refusal is attributed to NAME/ADDRESS when the text passes with those two kinds excluded. ## 9. Order 11(5): Intake's plain account number without a context word
Read and probed in the snapshot (round-2 probe D, bare `redact()`, no map, made-up digits):
| digits | standalone | before a full stop | a letter directly before | after a hyphen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8-9 | kept | kept | kept | kept |
| 10, 11, 13-16 | masked (PHONE) | **kept** | kept | kept |
| 12 | masked (ACCOUNTID) | masked | kept | masked |
| 17-18 | kept | kept | kept | kept |
PHONE (redact.py 478-485) is `\+?\d(?:[\d \-]){8,14}\d`: 10 to 16 characters, digits with spaces or hyphens inside and a digit at each end; it must not follow a letter, digit, `_`, `.` or `-`, must not be followed by a letter, digit, `_` or `.`, and is not an ISO date or a 14-digit compact timestamp. (The probe column tests a letter BEFORE the digits only.) ACCOUNTID (360) is `\b\d{12}\b`. BANKACCT (338-356; `_ACCT_WORD` 89 accepts "account no", "acct", "A/C"; 6 to 17 digits, `_ACCT_DIGITS` 93-96) is masked only with bank context: a phrase of `_BANK_CONTEXT` (100-110, e.g. "bank account", a bank code) within `_BANK_WINDOW` = 100 characters (111, `_bank_context` 114-120), or the match starting with "bank", or in `redact_many` and the fold scan a bank word anywhere in the batch/text (`bank_near`, 1056-1059, 1535). "Account No:" and "A/C" are keywords of the pattern but NOT context. HIGHENTROPY needs 40 or more characters with upper case, lower case and a digit (643-650), so a digit string is never caught. IBAN (253-260) is a registry country code, 2 digits and 11-30 letters or digits, with no check-digit validation and no context word. `_redact_value` returns int and float unchanged (runmap.py 590-600): a JSON-number account number is never scanned.
