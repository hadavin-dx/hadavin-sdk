## 1. The question, in plain words
A value V that the redactor already knows is replaced by a placeholder when written plainly. The same V written with look-alike letters, other case, one combining mark, zero-width characters inside, full-width forms, or split over two lines goes to the model unchanged. What is the smallest design that makes such a copy impossible to send?
**One answer.** Any copy WITHIN ONE TEXT (or within one `redact_many` call) that a fold of the text still reads as V makes the redactor REFUSE the whole text (the check, section 5). A copy split across two separate texts (its head in one tool result, its tail in another) is not read (section 10; ADV-LK6-9). Draft 5's second answer, a case pass that masked other-case copies of stored names and addresses in place, stays DELETED (round 5; order 8).
**What "a folded copy" means in this note (exactly):** a text one of whose scan keys contains a scan key of V. The scan key is `scan_keyset` of P1b as P1 defines it (the passes A and B in P1's item "P1b: identity, the readings, the keys"; the class maps, the `str.isalnum()` removal and the tag texts T0 to T3 in P1's item "P1b: the DENY keys (A1: no choice point left in them)" (draft 10, F1); read by name; its entity step is cut since revision 12, lead order 14(1), and its only decoding is the tag texts T1 to T3): tag texts T0-T3, NFKD and the confusables table, case folded in two readings, class maps `z` to `3` and `l` to `i` after the casefold, then every character for which `str.isalnum()` is False removed (whitespace, punctuation, symbols); the same steps run on V and on the text. The text is read as written AND after `runmap.undo` (JSON, backslash, percent and entity layers decoded), each read with the count rule of section 5 item 3. Anything a reader can see as V only through a transformation outside these functions is NOT covered (section 10).
**Scope, stated plainly.** (a) This closes the table for the core repository (`hadavin_sdk`). Warden's own `redaction.redact` is not fixed in the Warden repository (lead order 13(2)): when Warden is promoted, its redaction module is REPLACED by the core one (the retire list), so the fix for Warden is this check; section 8 gives the retire-list line and the Warden handover ticket text. (b) Stored NAME and ADDRESS values follow the evidence rule of lead order 13(1) (section 7, precondition P-1): if benign-document refusals with NAME and ADDRESS checked are at or below 1 in 200 texts, they are IN the v1 check; if above, they stay out, and the gap is one CHANGELOG line plus a row in the lead's later-fixes list. Two outcomes only, IN and OUT, as order 13(1) says (draft 6's middle step IN-GATED is deleted, ADV-LK6-6, COR-LK6-3). Until P-1 runs, which of the two P2d-2 builds is NOT decided; the code is built after P-1, for the outcome P-1 gives. (c) WHOLE copies of every other known value (secrets and identifiers) are covered on every path in the table when the value has a raw length of 12 or more AND at least one of its scan keys has 8 or more letters and digits (its shorter keys are dropped, section 5 item 1; COR-LK7-5). This is NARROWER than order 11's "12+ characters": a 12-character value of which 5 or more characters are not letters or digits (a password made mostly of punctuation) is not covered (ADV-LK6-4; PL-LK-36, ACCEPTED by lead order 14(2)). **Said plainly (lead order 15, which corrects order 14(2)):** for secrets of 8 to 11 characters (common passwords), **only plain copies** are caught; a look-alike, other-case, marked or split copy of such a secret reaches the model. They are never described as covered. The case-folded sweep that would catch their other-case copies stays a v1.1 row (LKV-2, trigger "before any pilot with real client data", order 15). **Partial copies, said plainly (draft 10, M2; PL-LK-53): A copy of only part of a known value, or a copy with one character changed, added or dropped, is not caught: it reaches the model.** (The check looks for the value's WHOLE scan key; strict xfail LK-PART; LKV-18.) **Self-refusal, said plainly (draft 10, M1; PL-LK-52):** a checked stored value that holds a letter the two case readings read differently (such as Greek capital Nu or Eta) makes every text that holds its own, correctly masked placeholder refused (section 5 item 3, FR-42, FR-46). (d) **Encoded copies (lead order 14(1)):** a known value encoded in invisible characters (bits written as zero-width characters or variation selectors), in base64 or in hex is caught by nothing in v1, whatever its length (section 10; strict xfail LK-ENC; the lead logs it as a v1.1 item with the trigger "before any pilot with real client data").

## 3. Three designs compared
**(a) Span-mask.** Fold, find V's key, map the hit back to written positions, replace only that span. Needs a position map; `_aligned_fold` (redact.py 1082) gives one only for simple folds and returns None otherwise (COR-R4-4), and then the line is withheld anyway. P2d-1's alias index is a smaller version of this idea and drew findings in rounds 3 to 5 (ADV-R3-2, ADV-R3-3, ADV-R3-8, ADV-R5-5, ADV-R5-6). Best fidelity, largest surface. Rejected for v1 (v1.1 LKV-1).
**(b) Refuse the whole text.** After all masking, if a scan key of V occurs in a scan key of the text, refuse. No position map. A person looks.
**(c) The round-4 fallback (`deny_hit` over the final output).** Gone: `deny_hit`, `prepare_needles` and `id_keyset` are cut from P1 v1 (P1 rev 11 and 11, PENDING LEAD 27). Its useful part, the scan key, is `scan_keyset`, which (b) calls directly. GOLD-1 is an independent leak oracle that shares none of the check's reading rules (section 6).
**Pick: (b), and nothing else.** Why: every table row is closed by an `in`-style check on folded keys; the failure is a refusal, not a leak (fail closed); the span machinery that kept failing review is not needed. What it costs: the whole text is lost when it holds a variant of a known value, including an upper-case copy of a known secret. **On the agent-loop paths a refusal ends the run**: the document path reports `redaction_error` (agent_loop 376-377; only `InputTooLarge` is reported as `document_too_large`, 371-375), tool results 469-470, tool inputs 495-496, the proposal check 649-650, all through `_redaction_failed` (547-549: a `guard/redaction failed` note and an abstention). **On the degrade paths it becomes a fixed text**: `audit/capped.py` 72-73 (withheld marker), `tools.error_reason` and `safe_head` (`SAFE_HEAD_NOTE`), and the approval request summary (its existing fallback card, section 5). **The k1b case bundle (an eval replay, not an agent run):** a refused string aborts the whole bundle, as any `RedactionError` other than `TooDeep` does today (`k1b/redaction.py` 189 catches `TooDeep` only; `k1b/replay.py` 158 has no handler; the replay's `except BaseException` at 273 keeps the output folder from looking finished); nothing of that bundle reaches the model (draft 6's edit there is CUT, ADV-LK6-2).

## 4. Why a plain fold is not enough (decides the dependency)
My draft-1 prototype used the snapshot `fold` with case folding and all whitespace removed. All table rows were caught, but the table reads some look-alikes as a different character: Cyrillic Ze (U+0437) reads `3`, so a V holding `z` written with Ze is missed; Cyrillic Be (U+0431) reads `6`; small Greek eta (U+03B7) reads `n`. Confirmed in `_confusables.py`. P1b's class map (`z` to `3`) and two case readings close Ze and capital Eta. **So the check needs `scan_keyset`, and P2d-2 FOLLOWS P1b** (order 11; P1 rev 11 "What downstream packages wait for"). P1 keeps no class map for digit-shaped letters (PENDING LEAD 29, narrowed by PENDING LEAD 36 to digits): a known value that holds the DIGIT and is written with such a source is found; one that holds the LETTER and is written with a digit-shaped source (Be for `b`) is a leetspeak rewrite, not a look-alike, and stays open (LKV-3, xfail LK-BE). Class maps live in P1b only; this design adds none.
**What P1 revision 11 changes here (written; read by grep for this draft, COR-LK6-2):** the scan keys keep letters and digits only (`str.isalnum()`, so whitespace, punctuation, symbols, `?` and U+FFFD go) and map `l` to `i` after the casefold, on V and on the text alike. So dots, hyphens, underscores, `?` and U+FFFD between the letters of V are removed from the key, and capital I (or Cyrillic U+0406) written for small l reads the same: those copies are REFUSED (rows LK-FILL, LK-IL, LK-SURR, marked (rev 11)), and the key-length rule of section 5 counts after that removal. Revision 11 also says what is not folded: plain-ASCII digit look-alikes (`1` for `l`, `0` for `O`) and a letter in neither the Unicode data nor the generator's supplement (its "Stated limits" (11), row U+0138). `has_hidden_channel` keeps rules 1 and 2 only and both tiers are cut (PENDING LEAD 35, narrowed by the P1 round 10 resolve, PENDING LEAD 40; lead order 14(1)); this design never relied on rules 3 to 7 or on a tier. Revision 12 cut the entity step of `scan_keyset` (lead order 14(1)) and revision 13 keeps the cut ("P1b: other": no decoder in any view; read by name in round 8); rows LK-FILL, LK-IL, LK-SURR rest on P1's item "P1b: the DENY keys (A1: no choice point left in them)" (`l` to `i`, `z` to `3` and the `str.isalnum()` removal; draft 9 named the wrong item, F1), which revision 13 keeps (read by name in round 8; the item name checked by the round-9 resolve, fact 7).
**A class of letters the table drops (ADV-LK6-3; found and counted by this draft on the snapshot data).** The generator keeps a UTS #39 "MA" entry only when its target is entirely ASCII letters and digits (`tools/gen_confusables.py`, selection rules). The data gives many Latin letters with a stroke or bar as an ASCII letter PLUS a combining overlay, for example U+0127 `ħ` as `h` + U+0335, U+0167 `ŧ`, U+0180 `ƀ`, U+01B6 `ƶ`, U+0268 `ɨ`, U+0126 `Ħ` likewise; NFKD leaves them unchanged; so none of them is in the 677-entry table (only the supplement's U+00F8, U+0142, U+0111 are). Count on the snapshot data: 177 MA entries (148 of them letters) whose target, with its combining marks removed, is ASCII letters and digits, all dropped. U+0289 `ʉ` has no MA entry at all. A copy of V with `ħ` for `h` is therefore SENT today and under P1 revision 11 as written. The fix belongs to P1's generator (one selection rule: test the target after removing Mn and Me characters, plus a property test over the data; section 8); until P1 takes it, row LK-STROKE is a strict xfail here (it flips to refused, like LK-IL, when P1 does).
From P1 revision 12, P1 supplies no decoder at all (lead order 14(1): the entity step of `scan_keyset` is cut; one decode path, `runmap.undo` beside each text, instead of two that disagree). Every decoded form therefore comes from this design, through the existing `runmap.undo` (runmap.py 139-170), on BOTH sides: each checked text is read as written and as `undo(text)` (section 5 item 3), and each checked original as written and, when different, as `undo(original)` (section 5 item 1; new in draft 8: a stored value holding entity-like text such as `x&notY`, shown decoded as `x` + the `¬` sign + `Y`, was caught by the entity step on both sides and would be lost by the cut, P1 round 10 honest note (c); row LK-ENT-VAL). **What `undo`'s fixed point loses (RES-R10-3, relayed by the P1 round 10 resolve, its fact 2; read, not run for this draft):** `undo` repeats its decoders until nothing changes, so a `&` standing right before a value whose letter is written as a numeric entity can join the decoded letters into one of the 106 entity names that `html.unescape` decodes without a semicolon: `&&#108;tq-...` gives `&ltq-...` in round 1 and `<q-...` in round 2, so a known value HOLDING `ltq` anywhere (not only at its start; also `gt`, the upper-case names `LT` and `GT`, and every other of the 106 names such as `amp`, `not`, `reg`) loses those letters in the decoded form, while the written form reads the digits `108`. Neither form finds that copy: strict xfail LK-ENT-AMP, LKV-15. By the reviewer's arithmetic (not run) a few percent of random secrets of 16 or more letters and digits hold such a name. Reading every `undo` round as its own form would close it, but costs one `scan_keyset` call per round (about 2 s each at the size cap, UNVERIFIED), which does not fit the 6.0 s budget; stated, not built (PL-LK-46). **The cheaper pre-pass (round 8, M-2; priced and run on the snapshot `undo`, round-8 resolve fact 5):** dropping, in the check only, every `&` not followed by `[#\w]+;` before `undo` costs 5 ms of CPU on a 262,144-character text with 43,691 ampersands and finds the `&&#108;tq` and `&%6Ctq` copies; but it does NOT find the same copy with the `&` itself encoded (`&#38;&#108;tq`, `%26&#108;tq`, a JSON `\u0026`), because that `&` appears only after the first decoding round, and it LOSES a copy today's `undo` finds (a made-up accented value written with the no-semicolon entity `&eacute` followed by more letters). It swaps one hole for another and is NOT built (PL-LK-51).
The design uses no other P1 function: `squeeze` and `fold` are not called; P1 keeps both public for their own callers (PENDING LEAD 37).

## 5. The design (b), exactly
**New public function** `check_lookalike(text, mapping, *, extra=(), _cache=None, _deadline=None) -> None` (ONE text, a `str`; every caller passes one: the final `out` of `redact_text`, the output of bare `redact`, the joined `redact_many` outputs; draft 8's list parameter is cut, F14) in `guard/redact.py`; it raises **`LookalikeCopy`**, a new public subclass of `runmap.RedactionError` defined in `runmap.py` and raised from `redact.py` through a function-level import (the precedent: `_Redactor._over_budget`, redact.py 986-992, raises `InputTooLarge` the same way; `runmap.undo` is imported the same way). Message = class name only. Bare callers that catch `RedactionError` catch it. **Every textnorm call of the check (`scan_keyset` on each original, on each text and on its decoded form) is made in the body of `check_lookalike` itself, and its only exception handler, which names `TextnormError` and counts a hit, is there too**: `check_lookalike` is one of the THREE v1 entries of P1's `REFUSAL_HANDLERS` (P1 rev 11, "the caller rule and the refusal lint": `guard/redact.py::check_lookalike`, the P2d-2 withhold function, the P5a mapper); no helper and no memo function calls textnorm (ADV-LK5-8). The search (item 2) is plain `in` in the same body and calls no textnorm function. There is no `except Exception` around a textnorm call (lint (a) has no exemption; COR-R8-9).
1. **Values and keys.** Every original of `mapping` except `"\0seed"` entries (round-5 resolve: kept; FR-13 now has an input that kills its removal) and except, in outcome OUT of P-1, originals whose kind is in `defaults.LOOKALIKE_WORD_KINDS = ("NAME", "ADDRESS")` (below), plus every string in `extra` (treated as a secret; never added to the map). There is no list of included kinds: a kind added to the redactor later is checked by default (ADV-LK4-9). **One length rule for every kind:** the RAW original has at least `defaults.LOOKALIKE_MIN_RAW_CHARS = 12` characters (`len(original)`, spaces counted) AND at least one key of `scan_keyset(original)` has `defaults.LOOKALIKE_MIN_KEY_CHARS = 8` or more characters, counted after the key's removals; each key under 8 is dropped (COR-LK7-5). **Needle forms (new in draft 8):** when `runmap.undo(original)` differs from the original, its keys are needles too, under the same rules (row LK-ENT-VAL, mutant M53); they are needles ONLY and never enter the explained count `e` of item 3 (draft 9, B-1); a `TooDeep` or an `InputTooLarge` from `undo(original)` (the second from `fits_after_nfkc`, which `runmap.nfkc` calls, runmap.py 99-107 and 195: an original whose NFKC expansion is over the cap) counts like an unfoldable original: a hit (draft 10, N4; row LK-UNFOLD). So a secret of 12 or more characters, a 36-character UUID, a 14-character id written with spaces are covered; an 11-character SSN-shaped value, a 10-character PAN, any secret of 8 to 11 characters, and a 12-character secret of which 5 or more characters are not letters or digits are not (xfail LK-SHORT; FR-6b; LKV-2; said in the owner summary, ADV-LK5-13). Keys are built once per original and kept in a per-run memo `cache["lookalike_keys"]` (in the run's cache, never module-level, R371). **What the memo keeps (draft 10, M3; PL-LK-54): per original, ALL keys of `scan_keyset(original)` (the UNFILTERED list) and, separately, the needle keys (8 or more); or the marker "unfoldable". The length filter applies to needles only; `e` (item 3) reads the unfiltered list.** Every put-back original is folded once into the memo, short ones (under 12 raw characters, never needles) included. **An original that `scan_keyset` refuses (`TextnormError`) counts as a hit: the check raises `LookalikeCopy` for every checked text while that value is in the map** (fail closed; P1 error contract, ADV-R8-5, P1 PENDING LEAD 34 (P1's item; this design's own PL-LK-34 is a different item, F11); row LK-UNFOLD). This includes a put-back original that is not itself checked (a short value whose token stands in the text): its `TextnormError` counts as a hit too, and that `scan_keyset` call is in the body of `check_lookalike` like every other (P1's `REFUSAL_HANDLERS`). **"The map" is `current`, the map as this call has grown it (the values the patterns found in this very text included), not the `mapping` the caller handed in** (mutant M48). Consequence (ADV-LK6-8, read): a value FOUND in a text and refused by P1's fold refuses that same text, and it is never stored, because `redact_text` updates the caller's map only after a pass (runmap.py 526-527) and bare `redact` works on a copy of the map (redact.py 953) that it returns only on success. So a planted unfoldable value cannot make every later check of the run fail; only a value that a caller puts into the map itself (the MAP way) can, and then every check while it is held refuses (stated, section 10).
   **Word kinds (NAME, ADDRESS): built for the outcome of P-1 (section 7; lead order 13(1), two outcomes).** Outcome IN: no exclusion, `LOOKALIKE_WORD_KINDS` is not added, word kinds are checked like every other kind. Outcome OUT: word-kind originals are skipped (`LOOKALIKE_WORD_KINDS = ("NAME", "ADDRESS")`). Effect: IN closes every table row for names and addresses and refuses benign texts the way P-1 measured; OUT sends every variant copy of a stored name or address (rows LK-WORD, LK-WORD-ASCII). Draft 6's middle outcome IN-GATED (a gate character and a 16-character exempt set) is DELETED (ADV-LK6-6, COR-LK6-3): it was the round-5 resolve's own addition to an order that names two outcomes, it needed three builds, and it had a hole of its own (a no-break space or U+3000 between the parts of a name opens no gate). **Nothing is masked in place any more for these kinds:** draft 5's case pass is deleted (it masked `BACKUP_DIR` as `<NAME_1>_DIR` after `username: backup` and restored `backup_DIR` into a tool call, ADV-LK5-2; and a `re.IGNORECASE` match cannot be mapped back by a case-folded lookup, ADV-LK5-4, fact 3).
2. **Search: plain substring search (`str.count`), no finder (ADV-LK6-7; draft 6's finder is CUT unless P-2 brings it back).** The keys of all checked originals are collected into one dict key -> label (deduplicated: equal keys from two originals are searched once; the label is the placeholder of the first original, longest first, then insertion order, or `<HANDED_SECRET>` for an `extra` value; used only for the log line, never the value). For each checked form and each of its keys `s`, the check computes `s.count(k)` for every needle key `k` (the count rule of item 3; for a form without a hit this costs the same as `k in s`), and checks the deadline (item 4) before each original's keys. No regex is compiled, so `_build`, `_compile_uncached` and the sweep are not touched and there is no finder cache that could go stale (draft 6's `cache["lookalike"]` snapshot rule, row LK-CACHE and mutants M11 and M12 are deleted with it). Only the per-original key memo `cache["lookalike_keys"]` stays: it maps an original string to its keys, which depend on that string alone, so it cannot go stale. With a plain dict and no cache (bare callers), keys are built on every call and nothing survives the call (row LK-NOCACHE, mutant M39). **Cost (measured on made-up strings, round-6 resolve; not the final code):** one `in` over a 262,144-character key takes about 0.1 ms of thread CPU (4,000 tests 0.38 s; 32,000 tests 3.1 s; a worst-case needle about 1.1 ms). A benign near-cap text with a 500-entry map makes about 500 x 2 needle keys x 2 text keys x 2 forms = 4,000 tests (about 0.4 s); a hostile 2,000-original map with 8 keys each against 8 text keys in 2 forms makes about 256,000 tests and is stopped by the deadline (`InputTooLarge`, fail closed, FR-8). P-2 measures the real figure and decides (section 7).
3. **Text, its decoded form, and the count rule (draft 8; replaces draft 7's restore form, ADV-LK7-1).** For each checked text t, the check reads t and, when different, `runmap.undo(t)` (JSON `\uXXXX` and one-character escapes, backslash escapes, percent-encoding, HTML entities, NFKC and invisible characters, to a fixed point; nested deeper than `defaults.SCRUB_MAX_LAYERS` it raises `TooDeep`, a `RedactionError`, which propagates: fail closed). In each form, every **genuine placeholder** (a token matching `redact._PLACEHOLDER` whose entry is in the map and is not a `"\0seed"` entry) is replaced by its ORIGINAL, and the check keeps a tally of how many placeholders of each original it put back. **The put-back is ONE pass (one `_PLACEHOLDER.sub` over the form; draft 10, N5): a token-shaped text inside a put-back original stays as written and is not put back again** (an original can hold such text only by the MAP way or as a token not in the map, redact.py 1226-1229; row FR-45, mutant M62). This is the **count form**. A seed placeholder, and any `<UPPER_n>` token that is not in the map, stays as written and is read as ordinary letters (COR-LK7-8; LK-FAKEPH: today `sweep` skips every such token, so `<ABCD1234EFGH_5>`, an upper-case spelling of an alphanumeric value, survives in model-written text and on bare paths; on `redact_untrusted` such a token is first rewritten with `‹ ›` by `escape_placeholders`, runmap.py 320-325, whose angle quotes the scan key removes; either way the letters are read; COR-LK5-11). The form goes through `scan_keyset` once. **Length bound BEFORE anything is put back (draft 9, F3):** the check first computes, from the placeholders it finds and the map, the length the form would have (`len(form)` plus, for each genuine placeholder, `len(original) - len(placeholder)`); when that length is over `defaults.TEXTNORM_MAX_CHARS` (262,144, P1's size constant; no new constant) the text is REFUSED (`LookalikeCopy`) and nothing is put back or built (row FR-41, mutant M59). **The bound is on `len`, on purpose, NOT on P1's W(text)** (draft 10, N7, adversarial A3; W(text) is the sum of w(c), "the longest string the pipeline makes from the single character c", P1 item "Input rules (P1a): type, then ONE size rule"); the P2d author does NOT compute W. Where W is larger than `len` (non-ASCII originals), P1's size rule refuses later with `TextTooLong`, a hit (FR-41 input (c)). If w(c) can be 0 for a character the pipeline drops (P1's definition does not say; UNVERIFIED), the bound can refuse a form P1 would accept: availability only, the safe side. Without it a model-written text of 23,831 placeholders of one 100,000-character value would make a string of about 2.4 billion characters before any size rule or deadline applies (1,000 placeholders made 100 million characters in 0.33 s of CPU, run, round-8 resolve fact 4). **Hit rule:** for each key `s` of the form and each needle key `k` (item 2), `c = s.count(k)` (non-overlapping); when `c > 0`, `e` = the sum over the put-back originals O of (the number of O's placeholders put back) x (the SMALLEST `count(k)` over the UNFILTERED keys of `scan_keyset(O)`, O as written: the memo's full list of item 1, keys under 8 included; **an empty key list counts 0**, never a bare `min()` over an empty list, which raises `ValueError`, not a `TextnormError`, and would refuse every text holding a needle and a short value's token as a `RedactionError`; draft 10, M3, rows FR-44 and LK-TAG-PB, mutants M60 and M61); the text is REFUSED when `c > e`. **Two rules for `e` (draft 9):** (i) O is put back AS WRITTEN in both forms (in the decoded form too: `undo(t)` is computed first, the put-back originals are not decoded), so `e` counts only O's own keys; the keys of `undo(O)` are needles, never explanation (B-1, run: in draft 8 a value V holding `%2B` stood as its own placeholder next to its decoded copy, plain or Cyrillic, and the key of `undo(V)`, which the written V does not hold, was "explained" by V's placeholder, so the copy PASSED on `redact_text` and on bare `redact`; the same with a model-written token of a value O whose decoded form holds W twice, next to a Cyrillic copy of W; draft 9 refuses all three and passes the controls; rows LK-ENT-VAL-PH, LK-ENT-VAL-X, FR-2c; mutant M57); (ii) the SMALLEST count, not the largest, because `scan_keyset` returns a `frozenset` with no label saying which case reading made which key, so "the count in the same reading" cannot be computed without a P1 change (M-1, run with a two-reading stand-in: a stored value X of V twice with capital Eta for `h` holds key(V) twice in one reading and not at all in the other; draft 8 let X's placeholder explain a copy of V written with Eta for `n`, found only in the other reading; draft 9 refuses it; row LK-ETA-COUNT, mutant M58). **The cost of (ii), said plainly (draft 10, M1/F4; PL-LK-52; draft 9 priced it too narrowly):** a CHECKED stored value (raw 12 or more and a key of 8 or more) holding a letter that the two case readings read differently has its own correctly masked placeholder refused, so every text that holds its token is refused (its own needle, the key of one reading, occurs once in the text; the smallest count over its keys is 0, because the other reading's key does not hold it). Such letters include the Greek capitals Beta, Zeta, Eta, Mu, Nu and Upsilon and the Cyrillic capitals Yeru, Soft sign and Yu (152 code points on a stand-in of the old fold, round-9 resolve fact 2; UNVERIFIED on P1b); in outcome IN this includes every Greek name or address starting with or holding one of them. A stored value with letters in tag characters is refused the same way (its tag-dropping key does not hold its tag-decoding key; round-9 resolve fact 5; such a value is a hidden channel anyway, P1's `has_hidden_channel` rule 1). Rows FR-42, FR-46. An ASCII value has one reading and is not affected. The self-needle exemption (count a needle that is itself a key of its own put-back original once per token) would remove this cost but is NOT built: it is unsound (run, round-9 resolve fact 3: a value with capital Nu and a copy with capital Eta at that place have equal keys in opposite readings, so the exemption passes the copy next to the value's token). P-1 measures how often it happens (section 7). In words: **a known value's key that occurs more often than the masked values put back can explain is a copy the redactor did not mask whole.** What it gives: a plain copy of V masked as V's placeholder is put back and explained by itself (FR-2, from a map holding V and from an empty map); two plain copies are explained twice (FR-2b); a stored value that holds V (an ARN that holds a 12-digit account id) explains the copy of V inside it (FR-37); a copy masked whole as a NEW value, as the folded scan does for a Cyrillic or zero-width copy of a long pattern-shaped V (redact.py 975-981, run), is put back and explained by that new value, whose key equals V's. But when a pattern masks only a PIECE of a copy of V, the piece is put back, V is read whole, and no put-back value holds V's key: REFUSED (rows LK-PH-FRAG, LK-PH-NESTED, LK-PH-SWALLOW, LK-PH-SWALLOW-NAME, LK-PH-UNDO). **Hand trace of ADV-LK7-1, run on the stand-in over the snapshot code (round-7 resolve, facts 1 and 2):** V is a made-up 60-character value of letters and digits, known from `password=<V>`. Draft 8 pinned it as a "not refused" row; draft 9 made it the strict xfail rows LK-INSERT and LK-INSERT-SHORT (PL-LK-48); draft 10 merges the second into LK-INSERT as its third input (the inserted e-mail under 12 characters gives the same result under the count form, F10; PL-LK-55), and the CHANGELOG says it (section 10). 4. **Fail closed and budget.** `TextTooLong`, `NotText` and any other `TextnormError`, from the text or from an original, count as a hit (`LookalikeCopy`). This includes a benign non-ASCII text that the redactor accepts but whose NFKD expansion is over `TEXTNORM_MAX_CHARS` (accents about x2, some Hangul about x3; P1 item "Input rules (P1a): type, then ONE size rule"): with a non-empty key set it is refused (row FR-33; P-1 counts it; COR-LK5-6, ADV-LK5-12). Every other exception propagates: `redact_text` turns a non-`RedactionError` into `RedactionError` (its existing handler around the `redact()` calls); a bare caller gets the exception and no text. `scan_keyset` has no clock (P1 rev 11 SK-4): the budget `deadline` (the caller's `_deadline`, or for a bare call one the check starts itself, `time.thread_time() + REDACT_BUDGET_S`) is checked before `undo`, before every `scan_keyset` call, before the `in` tests of each original's keys (item 2) and before each put-back original's `count` calls inside the hit rule's loop (item 3; draft 10, N6), and past it the check raises `InputTooLarge` (mutant M47: the deadline never checked; killer FR-8). `REDACT_BUDGET_S = 6.0` is CPU seconds of the calling thread (`time.thread_time()`, runmap.py 484); the `defaults.py` comment that says "wall-clock" is wrong and P2d-2 corrects it. The longest uninterrupted step is one `scan_keyset` call (S_call, measured by the P1b evidence runner; about 2 s by multiplication, UNVERIFIED), or one `undo` of a text at the cap (UNVERIFIED). Added work per `redact_text`: at most 2 checked texts (`out` and `undo(out)`) x one `scan_keyset` call, plus one `scan_keyset` call for each new original whose `undo` differs (once per run, memo). The check shares the redactor's budget. Today a benign text at the cap already takes 2.6 s (many IP addresses) to 4.0 s (a zero-width-split `password=`) of the 6.0 s (defaults.py 34-37, measured on a laptop, 2026-11-02), and `channel.clean` makes two `redact_text` calls; whether the check fits is measured BEFORE P2d-2 is built (section 7, precondition P-2, with its rule written down now).

## 7. False-refusal rate and cost: what is and is not known, and two preconditions
Not measured on real documents; no figure is guessed. Known: (1) after masking, a PLAIN copy of V is normally a placeholder. The sweep leaves plain copies in three production cases (an identifier inside a longer ASCII letter/digit run, words in `_COMMON`, values under 3 characters or all-digit under 6); only the first produces refusals (`_COMMON` words are at most 10 characters and short values are under the minimum). (2) Chance matches are negligible for random secrets and ids: the draft-1 prototype found 0 hits for 8,000 random 12-40-character keys in 262,144 characters; by arithmetic, a random 8-character key matches about 262,144 / 36^8 = 9e-8 per text per value, a 12-digit number about 2.6e-7 (revision 11's `l` to `i` map makes the alphabet 35, a change below the precision of these figures).
