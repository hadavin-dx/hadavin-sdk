## 1. The question, in plain words
A value V that the redactor already knows (12 or more characters) is replaced by a placeholder when written plainly. The same V written with look-alike letters, other case, one combining mark, zero-width characters inside, full-width forms or split over two lines goes to the model unchanged. What is the smallest design that makes such a copy impossible to send?

## 2. What I measured on the snapshot (throwaway script, nothing saved in the repository; UNVERIFIED by review; made-up 13-character value built from parts, so no value is printed here)
"Leaks" means the copy still stands in the returned text. Three ways of knowing V: a map handed in (MAP), an earlier `password=` call on the same run map (PW), `redact_many` (MANY). Paths: `runmap.redact_text` (the choke point), bare `redact` (public), `redact_many`.
| copy of V | `redact_text` MAP | `redact_text` PW | bare `redact` MAP | `redact_many` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain | masked | masked | masked | masked |
| Cyrillic look-alikes | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK |
| Greek look-alikes | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK |
| full-width forms | masked (canonical view) | masked | LEAK | LEAK |
| zero-width space inside | masked (canonical view) | masked | LEAK | LEAK |
| one combining mark | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK |
| upper case | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK |
| lower case | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK |
| split over two lines | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK | LEAK |
This matches the lead's run, with one refinement: the choke point already closes full-width and zero-width copies, because stage 2 of `redact_text` scans `canonical(text)` and adopts it when it holds more placeholders (runmap.py 509-523; fact V27 of the P2d ticket). Bare `redact` and `redact_many` have no stage 2, so they leak all eight. The cause is one fact: `_sweep` (redact.py 1485-1503) and `_replace_standalone` are literal, case-sensitive searches on the written text.

## 3. Three designs compared
**(a) Span-mask.** Fold the text, find V's folded key, map the hit back to written positions, replace only that span. Needs a position map. `_aligned_fold` (redact.py 1082) gives one only for texts the fold changes in simple ways and returns None otherwise (ligatures, Hangul jamo; COR-R4-4), and then the line is withheld anyway. P2d-1 rev 5 already carries an "alias index", a written-slice decision, 14 rows and 6 mutants for a smaller version of this idea, and it drew 17 findings in rounds 3 to 5 (ADV-R3-2, 3, 8, ADV-R5-5, 6, COR-R5-3, 4). Fidelity is best (the rest of the text survives). Surface is the largest. Rejected for v1.
**(b) Refuse the whole text.** After all masking, if any key of V's look-alike fold occurs in the folded text, refuse. No position map. A person looks. One function, three call sites, no change to what the patterns or the sweep do.
**(c) The round-4 fallback.** One `deny_hit` over the final output, with a `PreparedNeedles` built from the run's values. It is (b) built from P1b's public machinery. It costs what ADV-R4-2 and 3 found: a `BadNeedle` that fails the whole list on one short or odd value; the 500-needle cap and the hostile `"a"*63+"b"` pair that takes 2.3 s of the 2.0 s budget (P1 "Prototype cost"); the injected clock (`thread_time` default); sequential `in` searches (about 1.16 ms per needle at the cap, 9.3 s for 8,000); and the "k views" question. Rejected as built; (b) keeps its idea and drops that machinery.
**Pick: (b)**, built on two things that already exist: P1b's `scan_keyset` (the fold plus the readings) and the sweep's own trie finder (`_trie`, `_trie_regex`, `_compile_uncached`, redact.py 1353-1470). Why (b) and not (a): every table row is closed by one `in`-style check on folded text; the false refusal is a refusal, not a leak (fail closed, which is the studio rule); and the 17-findings-deep span machinery is not needed to close any row. What (b) costs: the whole text is lost when it holds a variant of a known value. Prototype timing (snapshot `fold`, made-up data, UNVERIFIED): fold plus whitespace removal of a 262,144-character text 0.04 s (ASCII) to 0.09 s (mixed scripts); building one trie finder over 8,000 random 12 to 40-character keys 0.33 s; scanning a 262,144-character key with it 0.10 s; 0 hits on random keys.

## 4. Why P1a's `fold` alone is not enough (found while prototyping; this decides the dependency)
My prototype used the snapshot's `fold` with case folding and all whitespace removed (what `scan_keyset` does, minus its readings). All eight table rows were caught on all three paths. But the fold table reads some look-alikes as a different character: Cyrillic Ze (U+0437) becomes `3`, so a V holding a `z` written with Ze is not found; Cyrillic Be becomes `6`; a small Greek eta reads `n` where V has `h`. P1b exists for exactly this: its class map `z` to `3` on both sides and its two case readings close Ze and capital Eta (P1 "P1b: the DENY keys"). So: **(b) needs P1b's `scan_keyset`; P2d-2 therefore waits for P1b again** (rev 5 removed that wait when it cut `deny_hit`). Residual after P1b, said plainly: letters whose look-alike the table reads as a non-letter and that have no class map (Cyrillic Be as `6`, small eta as `n`) are a stated limit with a v1.1 row (V17 below).

## 5. The design (b), exactly
**New function** `lookalike_hit(texts, mapping) -> str | None` in `guard/redact.py` (about 30 lines, no clock of its own; it reads the caller's deadline). Steps:
1. Values: every original of `mapping` except the `"\0seed"` entries, with length at least 3. Key set K = union of `scan_keyset(original)` for each, keeping only keys of at least `defaults.LOOKALIKE_MIN_KEY_CHARS` characters (proposal 12; length of the FINAL key, as P1 does for its minimums). The scan-key of a written-spelling original (the P2d-1 written slice) equals the key of the folded spelling by construction.
2. Finder: `_compile_uncached("(?=(" + _trie_regex(_trie(K)) + "))")`, built once per map length and kept in the redactor cache like `_build`'s state (`_built_for`, `cache["state"]`), never in `re`'s process-wide cache (R371 note at redact.py 1339).
3. Scan: split each text on genuine placeholders (`runmap.PLACEHOLDER`, as `sweep` does: a hit never spans a placeholder); for each segment take `scan_keyset(segment)` and search each key with the finder. A hit returns the placeholder of the matching value (for the log line; never the value).
4. A text that `scan_keyset` refuses (`TextTooLong`, `NotText`, any `TextnormError`) counts as a hit: fail closed.
**Call sites (the whole change):**
- `runmap.redact_text`: after the view loop and `_clean`, before `mapping.update`, on the final `out` AND on `again.text` of every view that stage 2 produced (adopted or not). Reason: a copy written with percent escapes or `\u` escapes is plain only in the decoded view; `scan_keyset` resolves HTML entities itself but not percent or backslash escapes (P1 "entity step"). Stage 1 and stage 2 `redact()` calls pass `_lookalike=False`, so a full-width or zero-width copy is still MASKED by the canonical view as today (V27) and is not turned into a refusal. A hit raises; `redact_text`'s existing `except Exception` turns it into `RedactionError("LookalikeCopy")`: nothing is shown, same contract as `InputTooLarge`. The shared budget applies: the finder build and the scans check the same `started + REDACT_BUDGET_S`.
- bare `redact` (when `_sweep_copies` is true) and `redact_many` (second pass): the check runs at the end and raises `LookalikeCopy(RuntimeError)`, a new class in `redact.py` whose message is the class name only. This is a behaviour change for bare callers (they got unmasked text; they now get an exception): CHANGELOG entry, loud-failure test.
- Not a call site: `sweep`. A check inside `sweep` would fire in stage 1 before the views and refuse what the views mask today.
**New constant** (`defaults.py`, hot file): `LOOKALIKE_MIN_KEY_CHARS = 12`. **No new public name** except the exception `LookalikeCopy` and the function (private, underscore: `_lookalike_hit`). **Kinds:** all stored kinds by default; a second constant `LOOKALIKE_KINDS` (proposal: all) lets the lead drop NAME, ADDRESS and TENANT if the measured false-refusal rate is too high (section 7).
**What it does not cover (stated, one line in the CHANGELOG):** a value not yet known when the variant text passes (the first sighting is never masked by any design); values whose final key is under 12 characters (v1.1 V16); text hidden in tag characters or unassigned code points that is not a copy of a known value (P2d-2's `has_hidden_channel` withhold, a separate hook; it should share the same refusal path and exception); letters in the Be/eta class (V17).

## 7. False-refusal rate: what is and is not known
Not measured on real documents, and I will not guess a figure. Known: (1) after masking, a plain copy of V cannot be in the text (it is a placeholder), so a hit means a variant of V; (2) chance matches are negligible for secrets and ids of 12 or more mixed characters: the prototype found 0 hits for 8,000 random 12 to 40-character keys in 262,144 characters, and the arithmetic for a 12-digit number is about 2.6e-7 per text per value; (3) the one real risk is **names and addresses**: an all-capitals copy of a stored NAME in a letter header ("DEAR ...") is a genuine leak to the model and is refused, which loses the document. This is the case a person is likely to hit often on Intake-style documents. Evidence step (runner): 200 made-up invoices and letters with stored names in plain, capitals and accented spelling; write the refusal rate to `handover/evidence/lookalike-1.md`. Gate proposal: more than 1 refusal in 50 documents removes NAME, ADDRESS and TENANT from `LOOKALIKE_KINDS` and opens v1.1 V15 (span-mask for those kinds, design (a)). Identifier kinds with the sweep's "standalone" rule (hostnames, IPs, ids) are checked without that rule, so a 12-or-more-character identifier that sits inside a longer token (plain, which the sweep leaves on purpose) is now refused; row FR-11 pins it and the evidence counts it.

## 9. Order 11(5): Intake's plain account number without a context word
Read in the snapshot: `_BANK_CONTEXT`/`_bank_context` (redact.py 100-118) require a bank word within 100 characters for BANKACCT; IBAN has its own pattern (line 248) with a real country prefix and needs no context word. So today core behaves as Intake does. The planned P5c (plan-v3 row, "redactor kind and masked model wrapper") adds no detector, so **P5c does not close the plain-account-number gap**, and neither does this fix (it only protects numbers the run already knows). Adding a context-free digit-string pattern would mask invoice numbers and amounts. Recommendation (PENDING LEAD): not closed in v1; add an acceptance row stating it as a stated limit; the route that works is to register the number as a known value from the field that holds it (the extract step), which this design then protects. UNVERIFIED: Intake code, and whether Intake's field list already registers account numbers.
