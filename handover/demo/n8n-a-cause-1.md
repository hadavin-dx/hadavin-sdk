# Variant a (ten at once): why 12 of 20 in R2 and 3 of 5 bursts with duplicates in R2b? (read of the raw rows, no run)
Written 06:06Z. Rule from the lead: state a cause only with evidence, else NOT ESTABLISHED. Rows read: `R2-tables.md` source rows (20 bursts), `R2b-a-5bursts.jsonl.txt` (5), the two fresh-stack control rows, and the published rows of the branch (20 valid bursts, `published_run/g12_a_default.ndjson`).

## Facts found in the rows (each is checkable in the files)
1. **Duplicates equal the number of "200" replies.** In every one of the 25 bursts of R2 and R2b, and in every one of the 20 published bursts, the number of CRM rows (and of emails) equals the number of clients that got reply 200 (for example 5 x 200 and 5 CRM rows; 10 x 200 and 10 CRM rows; 1 x 200 and 9 x 500 and 1 CRM row). So a duplicate is one of the ten parallel executions that passed variant a's first step as if the lead was new, and finished. The 500 replies are the executions that found the lead.
2. **How many pass is not constant.** R2: 1 row in 12 bursts, 2 in 1, 3 in 2, 5 in 3, 6 in 1, 7 in 1 (with 20 bursts). R2b: 1, 2, 10, 1, 2. Published: 1 row in 18, 3 in 1, 8 in 1. Fresh-stack control: 1 and 1.
3. **Load does not separate them.** CPU idle was 95.3 to 99.8 percent during all R2 and R2b variant a bursts (R2: 6 samples, lowest 95.3; R2b: 3 samples, lowest 97.3; control 97.8 to 99.8). No burst ran on a busy machine. Idle was sampled every 30 s, so a single burst is not matched to a sample exactly.
4. **Timing of the first execution partly points the same way, not enough to call a cause.** Time from the first execution's start to the first execution's stop (the moment the first CRM write could be visible): bursts with 1 CRM row 79 to 279 ms (14 of the 25 bursts, median about 140 ms); bursts with 2 to 7 rows 166 to 286 ms; the 10-row burst 1965 ms. The ranges overlap (166 to 279 ms occurs in both groups), so this does not separate the groups.
5. **The 10-row burst (R2b burst 3) was an odd burst.** Its ten executions started over 668 ms (the other bursts: 17 to 86 ms) and the first one stopped after 1965 ms. The machine was 97 to 99 percent idle in the samples around it. Why this burst was slow is NOT established.
6. **Send timing of the clients does not explain it.** Client send spread was 2 to 8 ms in every burst (R2 and R2b); the harness requires ten sent together.
7. **Replays added nothing** in all R2b and control bursts (the lead was then known).

8. **Added 06:40Z: the first burst after a fresh start was clean every time.** Fresh-stack control (one burst per stack): rows 1, 2, 3 (load-affected), 4, 5 = 5 of 5 at (1 CRM, 1 email). The FIRST burst of the two one-stack series (R2 attempt 1, R2b burst 1) was also (1,1). So 7 of 7 first bursts after a fresh start had no duplicate. The later bursts in the same stack: R2 attempts 2-20: 11 of 19 clean (8 with duplicates); R2b bursts 2-5: 1 of 4 clean. Published (20 valid bursts, one stack): the first row of the file is also clean; its 2 duplicate bursts were attempts 3 and 9. This is a pattern in the rows, not a cause: it is what a "state built up in n8n or the stand-in during a series" would look like, but it is also what 7 lucky draws at a 40 to 60 percent clean rate would look like only with chance about 1 to 3 percent (0.4^7 = 0.2 percent, 0.6^7 = 2.8 percent). Not tested further: a series of 3 bursts on a fresh stack where the 2nd and 3rd are checked.

## What is NOT established
- The cause of the difference to the published 18 of 20 (R2 12 of 20, R2b 2 of 5, published 2 duplicates in 20). Candidates that are NOT shown by these rows: machine load (rows say no), the harness patch P1 (it only waits before a restart; the first burst runs after that wait, so it could matter only through n8n's state after the restart; not tested), stack reuse (fresh-stack control 2 of 2 clean, too few).
- Whether it is n8n behaviour (how fast the first execution's CRM write becomes visible to the other nine) or the test stand-in's timing: the rows do not say. The files do not hold the stand-in's per-request timing for the CRM post.
- One way to settle it cheaply (not run): a burst series on 5 fresh stacks (ordered by the lead, running next) plus the same series after a restart with P1 and after a restart without any wait; the stand-in log would then show CRM write times per execution.
