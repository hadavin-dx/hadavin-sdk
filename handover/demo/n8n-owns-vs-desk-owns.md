# What n8n does and what the desk does (plain words; summary first, glossary last)

## Summary (11 lines; the glossary is at the end)
1. **n8n owns** (stated by the lead): intake, field mapping, the ten-at-once trigger, alerts, and plain sends in client builds.
2. **The desk owns** (repo text, mostly not tested by me): a receipt first, a keyed claim, ambiguous sends marked for a person, approval, its own counts, the live-model cost caps and on-screen masking. Redaction and the spend cap of the real-service checks sit in the lead's take harness (stated, not tested by me).
3. **Why the split:** these are the steps where a wrong answer costs money or trust. In my runs n8n's default duplicate node let duplicates through at ten-at-once on a reused n8n, while the variants that put the check in a database (b, c, d) did not; n8n's own documentation, as read by the lead, gives no exactly-once or at-least-once statement for webhooks, and the only documented uniqueness is the Postgres insert with Skip on Conflict plus a unique index (the guarantee then comes from the database).
4. **Variant a (n8n's default duplicate node), ten at once:** clean in 12 of 20 bursts on one reused n8n (published 18 of 20) and 2 of 5 in a second series; the first burst on a fresh n8n was clean 12 of 12, later bursts on fresh n8ns 8 of 10. Cause NOT established.
5. **Variants b, c, d** (database insert that ignores repeats, data-table upsert, one execution at a time): 20 of 20 clean each, reused n8n.
6. **A database rule vs check-then-insert (my runs: n8n 2.46.6, one machine, made-up data, ten identical leads at once with the same lead_id):** (a) A database rule (a unique index with the insert set to skip repeats) kept exactly 1 row in 20 of 20 bursts (5 fresh stacks and one reused stack of 5 bursts, two node settings). (b) The answer of that insert lets a flow tell a first call from a repeat: an IF box on it sent exactly 1 call down the "new" path and 9 down the "duplicate" path in 20 of 20 bursts (second set: 10 fresh stacks and one reused stack of 10 bursts). "New" only means this call's insert came first, not that the later steps finished. (c) The Data Table upsert kept 1 row in 10 of 10 (not documented as a guarantee). (d) A two-step Data Table flow (look first, then write, with no unique rule on the table) made more than 1 row in 9 of 10 bursts. For a client build: let a database unique index decide (the Postgres skip-on-conflict insert is the documented route); do not rely on a look-first-then-write check in a Data Table.
7. **Variant e** (b plus a status column and a re-send job): ten at once 25 of 25 clean, fresh n8ns; when n8n is killed at the two e-mail points a second CRM row came up in 1 of 50 (published 4 of 10) and the e-mail was sent again in every kill run.
8. **Killing n8n mid-flow, variant a:** 1 of 5 clean when the CRM request is in flight (published 5 of 5) and 3 of 10 at the e-mail points (published 9 of 10); b, c, d matched the published rows.
9. **Desk crash after the e-mail was sent (D2):** keyed mail system: one e-mail, follow-up `sent`; blind mail system: one e-mail, follow-up UNKNOWN (marked for a person). Three keyed runs (same result each), one blind run.
10. **Limits:** n8n 2.46.6 self-hosted, one Linux machine, a harness with my two scratch patches, the full run ended incomplete; Make never tested.
11. **Not claimed:** exactly-once delivery; "n8n alone is enough"; "n8n is broken".

## What n8n owns in the demo (stated)
Intake (form, e-mail, webhook), field mapping, the ten-at-once trigger, alerts, and plain sends in client builds: the steps of the flow and the stored logins to other services. The lead's order says they stay in the no-code tool so a client can change them.

## What the desk owns, and why (stated, with what I measured)
Two places, kept apart (both parts are STATED BY THE LEAD, NOT TESTED BY THIS LANE unless a row below says I ran it):
- **In the desk (`src/lead_desk`, public repo):** a receipt written before anything else happens, a keyed claim (a send key, so a repeated send is recognised by a keyed mail system), ambiguous sends marked for a person, the portal, approval of changed repeats, and a count of its own. I ran only the crash scene (D2) of these; I read the repo text for the rest.
- **Also in the desk's public code, from the repo's own text, not tested by me:** the live model step has a price, a per-run cap and a total cap (`src/lead_desk/live_claude.py`, `docs/limits.md`); it was run only against a local stand-in, no real model; on-screen masking of names, e-mail addresses, phone numbers and messages in the portal by default (promise P10 in `docs/tests.md`; the JSON replies still carry the values, and the browser tests are not run in the public checks).
- **In the take harness (the lead's laptop code: `run_real_check.py` address masking and scrubbing, `live_claude.py` spend ledger and cap in that worktree; not visible to this lane):** the lead says the redaction and the spend cap that matter for real-service checks are there. I did not read or run it. A text search of the public desk source finds no "redact" word, and finds "spend" or "budget" in ten files of `src/lead_desk` (for example `live_claude.py`, `config.py`, `contract.py`, `desk.py`); I did not read those hits.
Plain claim, not stronger (a person who wrongly marks a follow-up "not sent" can cause a second e-mail on a blind mail system, `docs/limits.md`; real mail, CRM and AI were not tested, the keyed mail system is a simulation): in the repo's tests, with its stand-in mail and CRM programs, the desk never sent a second e-mail by itself; the repo explicitly does not claim exactly-once delivery or "never twice" in general. Cases where it cannot protect: with a blind mail system a stop at D1b gives no e-mail and UNKNOWN; with a blind CRM a stop at C0 gives no CRM row, no e-mail and UNKNOWN; with a keyed mail system a repeat is safe only if that mail system honours the key.

## What follows, facts only
- **What n8n's documentation says (the lead's reading of the n8n docs on 2026-11-14, not checked by me):** no exactly-once or at-least-once statement for webhooks or queue jobs; Remove Duplicates and Data Tables say nothing about concurrency or atomicity; the only documented uniqueness is Postgres Insert with Skip on Conflict plus a unique index; the n8n API Idempotency-Key (from 2.45.0, on POST /api/v1 only) does not remove duplicate incoming webhook requests. Four n8n GitHub issues report one webhook call giving two or three executions: these are user reports, not facts, and I did not test them. My variant a duplicates are my own measurement and are not those reports. So variant c (data-table upsert) and variant d (one execution at a time) being clean 20 of 20 in my runs is an observation, not a documented guarantee; variant b's database check is the documented kind.
- n8n's default duplicate node let duplicates through at ten-at-once on a reused stack in my runs; the first burst on a fresh stack was clean 12 of 12 (counting the first bursts of R2 and R2b, which are also inside the 12 of 20 and 2 of 5; the split into "first burst" and "later bursts" was made after the rows were seen), but later bursts on fresh stacks also had duplicates (R2d: 2 of 10), and no significance is claimed. The cause is NOT established. So a claim "n8n alone is enough" cannot be made from these numbers, and neither can "n8n is broken": three alternatives (b, c, d) that put the one-time check in a database gave 20 of 20.
- The desk's two D2 rows are keyed three runs, blind one run. Their outcomes (keyed one e-mail; blind one e-mail and UNKNOWN) match the table in `docs/tests.md`; `contract.md` and `docs/limits.md` also report a real process kill at D2 in the repo's own tests (keyed and blind, and a third with a real mail catcher). The `sent` state, the counts-page number and exit code 137 in my rows are my own observations.
- **What the skip-on-conflict tests mean (my runs, n8n 2.46.6, one machine, made-up data, 10 at once; fresh or reused n8n as stated in the table):** the database enforces the one-row rule and the insert still answers with one item either way, so a flow can tell "new" from "duplicate" by whether the answer has a lead_id. "New" only means this call's insert was the first; it does not mean the later steps finished, so a retry after a crash would be labelled "duplicate". The two-step Data Table flow (check, then write, with no unique rule on the table) let extra rows in; a check followed by an insert that a unique index backs up was not tested. The Data Table upsert held in my runs but is not documented as a guarantee. One sentence for a client build: let a database unique index decide (the Postgres skip-on-conflict insert is the documented route) and do not rely on a look-first-then-write check in a Data Table. This does not show exactly-once for the later steps (CRM, e-mail) and says nothing about other n8n versions, other key types, more than 10 parallel calls or queue mode.
- Not tested: Make; any n8n version other than 2.46.6; n8n cloud; n8n in queue mode.

## Glossary
- **Variants** (tests of the same flow with the duplicate check done in different places; each is only a label here): **a** n8n's own default duplicate-removal node; **b** a database insert that ignores repeats; **c** a data-table upsert; **d** variant a limited to one execution at a time; **e** variant b plus a status column and a re-send job. **CRM**: the customer-record system. **Counts page**: the desk's page of totals. **Stand-ins**: small programs in the public repository that play the CRM, the mail system and the AI step. **Exit code 137**: the process was killed.
- **n8n**: the workflow tool where each step is a box. **Desk**: our small program (`src/lead_desk`) that sits behind it. **Fresh stack**: a newly built n8n plus database used for one test. **Reused stack**: one n8n that ran many tests in a row. **Keyed**: a mail system or CRM that recognises the same send key twice and acts once (the desk's "keyed claim" below is a different thing: it uses such a key). **Blind**: one that does not. **UNKNOWN**: with a blind mail system, when the desk cannot tell whether an email left, it does not guess, does not resend, and marks the follow-up for a person (with a keyed mail system an unclear end is retried with the same key instead). **Crash points** D1b, D2, C0: named moments in the desk's work where a test stops it; D1b is just before the mail system is called, D2 just after the e-mail was sent and before the desk noted it, C0 just before the desk writes to the CRM. **Ten at once**: ten identical lead submissions sent together.
- **Terms in the database lines:** **Postgres**: the database that stores the rows. **Unique index**: a database rule that refuses a second row with the same lead_id. **Skip on Conflict**: an insert setting that quietly ignores a refused row instead of failing (variant b uses it). **Upsert**: update the row if it exists, otherwise insert it (variant c). **Data Table**: n8n's own built-in table. **Serial id**: the database's automatic row counter. **lead_id**: the identifier of one lead. **Check-then-insert**: first look whether the row exists, then write it as a second step; two calls at once can both pass the look before either writes. **IF box (node)**: a step that sends the flow down a yes path or a no path. **Path**: one of those two ways out. **Node**: one box in a flow. **Webhook**: the web address that starts a flow. **Execution**: one run of a flow. **Reply**: the answer the webhook sends back. **Caller**: whoever sent one of the ten calls. **HTTP 500**: an error answer of the web. **Always Output Data**: a node setting that makes it pass on an empty item when it has nothing. **Burst**: ten calls sent at the same moment. **Created and updated times**: the two time stamps a Data Table row carries; equal means the row was just made.
