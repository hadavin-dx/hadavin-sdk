# Owner appendix v35, sheet B: the long detail (it binds you the same as the sheet; each part says where the sheet points to it)

## Appendix part 1: The words in full (every definition of the sheet)
**The words.**
- **Labels** R1 to R10 are the rules of the table in section 2, P1 and P2 the planned card moments, W3 to W9 the danger signs and L1 to L5 the lists on the card, each defined once there.
- **UNVERIFIED** also covers every vendor page, button, Windows, WSL, n8n and HubSpot step and every printed wording; the STOP RULE is at the end of sheet section 1.
- **Lead** and **numbers message**: as in sheet section 1. A **run name** is 4 to 12 lowercase letters and digits, starting with a letter, never used before. The **other channel** (a second way to reach the lead, not the message place) and the **backup person** (one named human you can call, who can reach whoever runs the lead; the only person you call when a cancel fails) are named in the numbers message; none named: no run.
- **WSL** = the Ubuntu window inside Windows (Start menu, type Ubuntu, Enter). The **test terminal** and **read terminal** (card words) are two WSL windows; the read terminal is for `date -u`, `ls -l`, `clear` and the grep of 5.7 only.
- **Send** = type in the message place and press Enter. **Describe** = send which step you are at, what kind of line you see and plain numbers. **Set phrase** = words you send exactly (the list is on the card page). **Run** as a verb = type a command and press Enter; a **run** = this one filmed attempt. **Key** alone = a model key; the words take in `take-` and `--take` are plain text (the name of this kind of run, nothing to take).
- **Words with one meaning here.** **Message** alone = the numbers message; the other kinds are named in full (lead message, clean-up message). **Take** appears only in fixed plain-text names (`take-<run name>` addresses, the Take folder, `--take`); a sheet is "used", not "taken". **Deadline** = only the end of your spending approval (Mon 23 Nov 2026 18:29 UTC = 23:59 IST); the program's refusal limit and the DEADLINE setting are always named in full. **None** = "there is no such thing" in every slot (no older key, no per-key limit, no cancel helper, no last-resort step; at 8.1 no time), and **never** = what the key list says of a key never used; **unknown** in a slot: no run. **Wait** = the lead's pause word (R8) or a normal wait; **gap** = only the minutes the numbers message gives before 8.1; **post** = the lead's word "post" and then your public post (section 9). **Cancel helper** = the person on the card blank who may cancel only the keys on their paper in the model account if you cannot (7.11, appendix part 10, page OWNER-HELPER-v35.md). A **cancel-only role** = a role in the model account that can press cancel on a key but cannot make keys or read key text (UNVERIFIED that the account offers one).
- **Model account** = the paid model company's account where you pay and make keys; open it only from your own bookmark or an address you type. **Key list** = its page that lists keys with name, creation time, last-used time and a **state word** (the word that says whether a key works). **Per-key limit** = a spending cap on one key, if the create form offers one (the message says "none exists" otherwise). **Key scopes** = the permission list on the create form (the message names the ones to choose). **Run day** is a UTC date: after 18:30 UTC = 00:00 IST your phone's IST date is already the next day. The **test program** (started by you) sends three made-up people through **n8n** (a ready-made linking tool) into **HubSpot** (a ready-made customer-record service; here a free test account): one **contact** (customer record) and one mail per person. **Valid key**, the rest: a state word you cannot judge counts as valid; an older key can be valid without being alive. **Look key** = a throwaway key `look-<run name>` you make at 5.9 only to see the real cancel words; it is a real key, alive until card step 4 is done for it. **Cancelled word** = the state word of a cancelled key (or "row gone"); the message gives a word or "from the look key" (the lead cannot see your screen); you see the real word at 5.9 and the lead answers "confirmed"; a word that differs from a given sample: no run.
- **Film pause** = the wait while the film person films; it starts at the **film cue** (a printed line copied in the numbers message, or minutes after you sent "run started"); you then call the film person in and say aloud "film pause now", which are the film person's start words. **Recording OFF** = every screen recording or sharing is off. **Privacy switches** = Windows Clipboard history, Sync across your devices, Recall; any clipboard manager, remote-access tool or screen-capture tool is off or closed the same way (never just minimised). **Film profile** = the Chrome profile `demo-take` (5.5). The numbers message names the film person, the **editor** (cuts the film) and the **post checker** (compares your post with the sentences); you are none of them.
- **Demo mail** = a mail in the receiver Gmail under label `demo` that R7 allows you to delete. **Throwaway accounts** = the receiver Gmail (gets the demo mails), the sender Gmail (sends them) and the HubSpot test account you made earlier, never your real mailbox. **App passwords** = Gmail passwords made for one application; **HubSpot app secrets** = secret values of the small HubSpot programs ("private apps") that n8n's workflows use to write contacts. **Shell prompt** = the line ending in `$` in an Ubuntu window where you can type a command. **The y question** = the line `Type y to start`. **Paste line** = `paste key, Enter:`. **Step** = a numbered item such as 6.3; **window** = a program window on your screen; **profile** = a Chrome profile.
- **Made-up people** = person A, B and C, with addresses `take-<run name>-a@example.com` (`-b@`, `-c@`); **submitted N times** = the test program sends that person's sign-up N times on purpose; **person id** = the made-up code the program gives that person (not an address); a printed line "unknown" for C is allowed (the program could not confirm that one); the **prep contact** = one extra practice contact. **Key file** = the file in WSL holding the model key. **Model program** = the lead's software that sends the made-up people's text to the paid model with your key; you never edit its settings. You never start or stop it: the lead does (UNVERIFIED how; it shares your Linux user, R1); **"restarted"** = the lead's word that it started again and now reads the key file. **Spend lock** = a lock that stops the model program making paid calls until the test program has accepted your challenge; "NOT SHOWN" = nobody has shown it exists, so the model program might spend between "restarted" and your `y` (the yes names this).
- **Money.** **Prepaid** = you pay first and calls draw it down; **auto-reload** = the account tops itself up when low (must be off). **Balance** = the whole prepaid amount: the most you can lose. **Last-resort step** = one action in the model account that only stops spending; the numbers message names it and says whether it can be undone (UNVERIFIED).
- **Times** (all in the numbers message). **Earliest key time**: before it you do not press create of the run key (the look key of 5.9 is made after "go", also before it). **Latest start** (card words; 7.3). **Take folder** = the WSL folder the message names (it holds the test program). **Run length** = the minutes the test program is expected to run from your `y` to the waiting line (the message states it). **Give-up minutes** = how long the test program waits for `end`, counted from your "run started", before it gives up. **Alarm time** and **cancel-by time** = when the run should be over, and the backup; **silence minutes** = how long the test terminal may print nothing before it counts as stuck. **FREEZE**, the rest: in the read terminal run only `date -u` and `ls -l "<path from the message>"`; in the browser do only the card (its list L2 included, at 7.11) and the deleting R7 allows; describe; wait for the lead's word "continue". FREEZE is not a cancel: a live key is cancelled only through the card (a planned moment P1 or P2, or a danger sign W3 to W9); a run whose key was alive ends after the card: no word resumes it. A FREEZE never stops 7.11: the older key is cancelled by the older-key time even while frozen (the card's list L2 is allowed in a FREEZE). "Continue" = the lead's word that you may go on to the next step (7.9 or 8.1) after a FREEZE in which no key was alive.

## Appendix part 6: Why and background (rationale that is not a step)
Step 5.1, a list that shows only a date: count that date as its last minute, so that date must be at least 8 days before the run day. Order: part a is a first look; the lead gives the run day in part b and you apply the rule again to it (5.6 repeats it); if it fails, ask the lead for sheet B. Why: sheet A cancels the older key at 6.7; a key used in the last 7 days probably still serves a program of yours, and the key list may show a last use hours late (UNVERIFIED), so 6.7 reads it again right before the irreversible cancel. Sheet B cancels the older key only at 7.11. When 6.7 did not cancel it (failed key save, decline, time passed, a use after your first reading), 7.11 cancels it by the older-key time even if it was used lately: money safety (R4) outweighs the use check. Money windows (the time the older key can spend besides your run key): sheet A from your press of create (6.3) to 6.7 on the happy path and to 7.11 on any failure path; sheet B always to 7.11. No older key: the rule does not apply; either sheet works.
