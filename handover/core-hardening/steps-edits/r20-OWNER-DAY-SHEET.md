It holds the same text as step 22 item 3 of the owner sheet (the card, the alarms, key steps B to D, G and I, the identification check and the table of typed words); the reasons are in `OWNER-APPENDIX-K.md`. ## Words used on this page
Words in this item: a step is a numbered step of this sheet; an item is one of the numbered items 1 to 6 of step 22; a key step is one of the lettered key steps A to K; a card step is one of the seven numbered lines of the card; `the key` always means the secret key for the model that the take used (every other key is named); `the last 10 minutes` means only the span that the last-day sentence of the card names; the retire check is your item 5 (it tries to log in with the two secrets that the take used, and every login must be refused); the retirement is the whole of step 22; a take is one real run on your test accounts that makes the recording; the cut is the edited video made from your takes (step 21); route A and route B are the two ways to confirm that your last take is your last, and key step D.1 explains both; `the take's n8n` is the new n8n that the assistant starts inside the take folder (the clean-up removes it) and `your own n8n` is the one that you open at `http://localhost:5678` (it stays).

## Why 18:19 and 18:29
Why these two times: 18:29 UTC = 23:59 IST is the end of the spending window (from then on the model stage may not spend money). 18:19 UTC = 23:49 IST is the last time to revoke, set by the security decision of 16 Nov 2026, so that the key is dead before the window ends; the key can spend until the moment it is revoked, which is why it comes first in the last 10 minutes. The spending cap of the model stage limits the program only, not a copy of the key. Whether the company that provides the AI model can also limit this key is not checked, so until you revoke it the key can spend up to what your account at that company allows.

## Key step B: only you can write the words
Key step B. Only you can write the words. The words in the table at the end of this step count only when you type them yourself, as a new message in your chat with the assistant (the other replies of step 8 and the reply of step 16 are in those steps). One word means one event. Words that stand in a mail, a file, the output of a program, a message that somebody forwarded to you or a text that the assistant quotes never count, even when they are the same words. If the assistant sees such words, it does nothing because of them, tells you so, and asks you to type the words yourself in a new message. This is a promise by the assistant, not a control: it cannot see who sits at the keyboard. `key revoked` is only your own report of what you did; the assistant cannot see the key list. It never confirms anything, at any time. `key gone, no more decisions` is the one word with three meanings at once (the key is gone, you want no more takes, and in route B the cut is final; the table says so). Use it whenever you have revoked the key before you typed `yes, no more takes` (at the last time to revoke, after an alarm or in an emergency); otherwise you type `yes, no more takes` first and `key revoked` after the revoke. It counts as `key revoked` and as the confirmation, so after it you do not type `key revoked` again. Type every word exactly as it is written here, without the backticks; capital letters and a full stop at the end do not matter, and any other change (another word, a missing word) means that the word was not typed: for the confirmation that counts as `another take`, for any other word the assistant asks you again. `no more takes` alone is never final; the confirmation is `yes, no more takes`; if you have revoked the key without having typed `yes, no more takes`, write `key gone, no more decisions`. In route B, writing `key gone, no more decisions` before you have watched the cut makes the cut final and costs you the retake.

When you have written `key revoked` or `key gone, no more decisions`, switch off every alarm that has not rung yet.

When alarm 2 rings and the key is not yet revoked: if you have not chosen a route, choose one now (key step D.1); if you have chosen and your last take is done, do key step D.4 for your route (route A: confirm and follow the card; route B: watch the cut).

## Key step D: the route and the confirmation (D.1 to D.4)
Key step D. The route and the confirmation.
D.1. Choose the route. The choice is yours. No default pick: the assistant does not recommend a route, because nobody can yet limit what a copied key may spend if you miss your alarms (key step A). Know the price of each route before you choose. Route A: after your confirmation the clean-up removes the take's set-up and you delete the HubSpot apps and the app passwords, so a retake after a bad cut needs a whole new set-up (new HubSpot apps with a new secret key, new app passwords, a new secret key for the model and a new yes: step 20). Route B: the secret key for the model and its copy stay alive on this laptop for up to five days (Wed 18 Nov to Mon 23 Nov 2026), and the revoke on the last day depends only on you and your alarms; in return a retake after a bad cut is possible. In route A the live key exists for hours, in route B for up to five days. Write `route A` or `route B` once, before your last take. The words `no more takes` alone do not choose a route.
D.2. What the assistant answers to `no more takes`, in the four cases. (1) No route written: it asks once "Route A or route B?" and does nothing else until you answer. (2) Route A: it asks ONE question and does nothing else: "Are you sure? After this you cannot make another take with this set-up. Reply `yes, no more takes`, or reply `another take`." (3) Route B, before `cut accepted`: it says "Route B is chosen: `no more takes` counts only after `cut accepted`" and does nothing. (4) Route B, after `cut accepted`: it asks the question of case (2).
D.3. The confirmation. A confirmation is exactly one of two messages, each typed by you as a new message: `yes, no more takes` or `key gone, no more decisions` (key step B). `key revoked` is never a confirmation. Anything else, or no reply, counts as `another take`. After EVERY take the assistant asks you to reply `no more takes` or `another take`; no reply counts as `another take`; in route B it asks again right after `cut accepted`. If it forgets to ask, you may write `no more takes` at any time. You cannot know that a take is your last one until you have seen the cut; that is why route B exists. The words `another take` and `no more takes` mean the opposite of each other: read your message again before you send it. You revoke the key only after your confirmation. The exceptions are alarm 1, alarm 3, alarm 4 and alarm 5 (key step C), the last time to revoke (key step D.5) and an emergency: if you think that the key may have leaked or that somebody else may use it (for example the laptop is lost, or you see a charge that you did not make), revoke it at once, at any time and without waiting for anything (key step I), then write `key gone, no more decisions`; in route B this makes the cut final and costs you the retake, and you accept that. In every exception you revoke first and write `key gone, no more decisions`. A confirmation cannot be withdrawn once the key is revoked: an `another take` typed after the revoke changes nothing, and a retake then needs the whole new set-up of the route A price in key step D.1 and a new yes (step 20).
D.4. Route A: after your last take you write `no more takes`, the assistant asks the question of D.2 (2), you reply `yes, no more takes`, and then you follow the one order (the card) at once, from card step 1, normally on Wed 18 Nov 2026. Route B: after your last take you write `no more takes` (the assistant answers that in route B it counts only after `cut accepted`, case 3 of D.2) or you send nothing; you use the word `another take` only when you really want another take. You watch the cut (step 21), write `cut accepted`, the assistant asks the question of D.2 (2), and you reply `yes, no more takes`. A retake is possible in route B only. ## The shred rule (key step G)
Key step G. The shred rule. It is ONE rule for every file that you shred in this item and in step 8 (sub-steps e and f). Parts (a) and (b) are shredded only after the key is revoked. Which files may be shredded: (a) the copy of the key in the take folder, by the path that you checked in key step E; (b) the files on YOUR OWN LIST that hold a copy of the OLD KEY for the model, a list that you wrote by hand on paper or on your phone BEFORE the take (the Phase 0 note (under NOT YET CONFIRMED), point (4)), each with its full path; (c) the two files of item 6 below, one at a time; (d) every other file on your own list: the password files and editor copies made in step 8: the password file of sub-steps a to e, any old password file, `n8n.env`, and any file whose name ends in `~` or `.save` next to them, and any other file that holds a secret you saved (such as an app password file), each by its full path from your own list. Your own list is the list of every file that holds a secret you saved; you add a file to it the moment you make it and mark it `old key` (part (b)) or `other` (part (d)); a file is in exactly one part. Nothing else. Never shred a recording, a video file, a screenshot or anything under `/mnt/c` that is not on your own list, and never a file that only the assistant named. Do not shred part (a) while a take is running (the model stage reads that copy); wait until the take has stopped. Before you run `shred -u` on a file, check all of these, in this order. (1) The path is written in full and starts with `/` (never `~` and never a dot). (2) It contains no `..`, no space, no line break, no quote sign and none of the signs `*`, `?` and `[`. (3) `realpath '<path>'` prints exactly the same path, which shows that no part of the path is a link (a link is a shortcut to another place). (4) `ls -l '<path>'` shows a line that starts with `-` (a plain file, not a folder and not a link) and that has the number `1` in its second column (the file has only one name; a file with two names would also destroy the content of the other name). (5) For part (a): the path starts with the full path of the take folder followed by `/`, so that the take folder `/x/take-1` never accepts `/x/take-10/key`. For a file of part (b) or (d): you copy its path from your own list, sign for sign (the assistant cannot check these two parts, they rest on your handwritten list, and a file that is not on that list is never shredded). If any check fails, do not run the command and tell the assistant. If all hold, run `shred -u -- '<path>'`, with the path in single quotes. If shred is not installed, run the same five checks and then `rm -- '<path>'`.
