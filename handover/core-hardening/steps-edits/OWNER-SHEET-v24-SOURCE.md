## 1. What happens, and the words
**The run in short.** You and the **lead** (the AI session in the chat where you got this sheet: an AI system, not a person) agree a **run name** (4 to 12 lowercase letters and digits, starting with a letter, never used before); you **tell** the lead things by typing them in that chat. The lead sends the **numbers message** (one message with every value for this run, sample in section 3); you check it by hand and type a yes, and the lead says "go". You make a fresh **model key** (a key to the paid model's account; it lets a program spend money) and save it in a file; from then the key is **alive**: it can spend money until you cancel it. You start the **test program** (the program you start by hand); it sends three made-up people through **n8n** (a ready-made tool that connects services without writing code) into **HubSpot** (a ready-made online service for customer records; here a free test account): one **contact** (a customer record) and one mail per person, asking the paid model at most once per person. A **film person** (a human who alone drives and films the browser) films the contact list and the mail list. You type `end`, cancel the key with **the card** (the one-page checklist of section 2, which you print) and prove it. You then delete and revoke what the lead lists, and a sentence is posted if the film is checked and everything agrees.

**The words** (each explained once, before it is used; a word keeps one meaning).
- **UTC** = world clock time. **IST** = UTC + 5:30; your phone alarms use IST. **UNVERIFIED** = nobody has run or seen it; where this sheet says so and what you see differs, stop (STOP RULE below), except the **tolerated** items (Windows menu paths, the Recall page, the paste method, the Chrome profile menu, the lid setting, the page paths for app passwords and HubSpot apps, where the contact id sits): tell in words what you saw and go on.
- **Message place** = the chat of the story, nowhere else; a message from anywhere else is not accepted. **Other channel** = a way to reach the lead outside the message place that you used before today; the numbers message names it; none named: no run.
- **WSL** = the Ubuntu window inside Windows (Start menu, type Ubuntu, Enter; again for another window). **Test terminal** = the WSL window where the test program runs. **Read terminal** = a second WSL window for read commands only (list at the end of section 2); open it at 5.1 and keep it open. `date -u` there prints the UTC date and time, for example `{{day_date}} 11:05:12 UTC 2026`.
- **Tell in words** = say which step you are at, what kind of line you see and plain numbers (example: a long text was on the screen, first 16 match). Never copy, type or photograph a key, an app password, any long random-looking text or a real person's mail address; no screenshots. **Set phrase** = words you tell exactly (list at the end of section 2).
- **Clean-up message** = the lead's message after the run, beginning "n8n is off", with the lists to delete and revoke; it comes only after you told "{{ph_mkc}}" or "{{ph_nokey}}".

If a key is alive, the card comes first, always.

**STOP RULE.** If at any moment you do not know what to do, or what you see differs from this sheet or the numbers message: (1) if a key is alive, do the card now; (2) then FREEZE and tell the lead in words. A paid key left alive costs money, so doing nothing is never safe while a key is alive. If two rules disagree: the card first, then R1, then R10, then the rest.
