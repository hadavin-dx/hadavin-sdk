## 1. What happens, and the words
**The run in short.** You and the **lead** type to each other in a chat; you make a paid key, run a test program once while a **film person** films, cancel the key with **the card**, then delete and revoke what the lead lists. The words below are each explained once, before use; a word keeps one meaning.

**The words** (each explained once, before it is used; a word keeps one meaning).
- **UTC** = world clock time. **IST** = UTC + 5:30; your phone alarms use IST. **UNVERIFIED** = nobody has run or seen it; where this sheet says so and what you see differs, stop (STOP RULE below), except the **tolerated** items (each step marks them): tell in words what you saw and go on.
- **Lead** = the AI session in the chat where you got this sheet (an AI system, not a person); you **tell** it things by typing them in that chat, which is the **message place**: nowhere else counts; a message from anywhere else is not accepted. The lead sends the **numbers message** (every value for this run, sample in section 3); you check it by hand, type a yes and wait for "go". A **run name** is 4 to 12 lowercase letters and digits, starting with a letter, never used before. The **other channel** (card words) is named in the numbers message; none named: no run.
- **WSL** = the Ubuntu window inside Windows (Start menu, type Ubuntu, Enter; again for another window). The **test terminal** and **read terminal** (card words) are two WSL windows; the read terminal is for `date -u`, `ls -l` and the grep of 5.7 only; keep it open from 5.1. `date -u` there prints the UTC date and time, for example `{{day_date}} 11:05:12 UTC 2026`.
- **Tell in words** = say which step you are at, what kind of line you see and plain numbers (example: a long text was on the screen, first 16 match). Never copy, type or photograph a key, an app password, any long random-looking text or a real person's mail address; no screenshots. **Set phrase** = words you tell exactly (list at the end of section 2).
- **Clean-up message** = the lead's message after the run (8.2) with the lists to delete and revoke.
- **Model key** = a key to the paid model's account; it lets a program spend money; it is **alive** from creation until you cancel it. The **test program** is the program you start by hand: it sends three made-up people through **n8n** (a ready-made tool that connects services without writing code) into **HubSpot** (a ready-made online service for customer records; here a free test account), making one **contact** (a customer record) and one mail per person. The **film person** is a human who alone drives and films the browser. **Older key** = a paid-model key you saved earlier, if the numbers message names one (its name and creation date). The **key list** also shows each key's last-used time. **Valid key** = a key the key list shows as usable. **Cancelled word** = the state word of a cancelled key, given in the numbers message (none given: no run).
- **Film pause** = the wait while the film person films; it starts at the **film cue** (a printed line copied in the numbers message, or minutes after you told "{{ph_rs}}"). **Recording OFF** = every screen recording or sharing is off and nobody else sees your screen. **Recording box** = the page area of the film profile's browser window (no tab strip, address bar or profile menu). **Privacy switches** = Windows Clipboard history, Sync across your devices, Recall. **Film profile** = the Chrome profile `demo-take` (5.5). The numbers message names the film person, the **editor** (crops and cuts the film) and the **post checker** (compares your post with the sentences); you are none of them.
- **Demo mail** = a mail in the receiver Gmail under label `demo` that R7 allows you to delete. **Throwaway accounts** = the receiver Gmail, the sender Gmail and the HubSpot test account you made earlier, never your real mailbox. **App passwords** are Gmail passwords made for one application; **HubSpot app secrets** are the secret values of the HubSpot private apps the flows use.
- **Made-up people** = person A, B and C, with mail addresses `take-<run name>-a@example.com` (`-b@`, `-c@`); the **prep contact** = one extra made-up contact the lead made for practice (the clean-up message lists its address). **Key file** = the file in WSL holding the model key. **Model program** = the lead's software that asks the paid model with your key; you never edit its settings file or the test program's.
- **Money.** **Prepaid** = you pay first and calls draw the amount down; **auto-reload** = the account tops itself up when low (must be off). **Balance** = the whole prepaid amount: the most you can lose. **Call ceiling** and **expected total**: see check 1 of section 3. **Last-resort step** = one action in the model account that only stops spending, named in the numbers message and in your yes.
- **Times** (in the numbers message). **Run day** = the one day of the run, as in `{{day}}`. **Deadline** = {{dl_day}} {{dl}}: after it nothing may spend. **Earliest key time**: before it you do not press create. **Latest start**: see the card (7.3). **Alarm time** and **cancel-by time** = when the run should be over, and the backup; **silence minutes** = how long the test terminal may print nothing before you treat it as stuck. **FREEZE** = type and close nothing more in the test terminal (except `n` at "Type y to start"); in the read terminal run only `date -u` and `ls -l "<path from the message>"`; in the browser do only the card and the deleting R7 allows; tell in words; wait for the lead's word "continue" (only for a FREEZE that began while no key was alive). If a key is alive, the card comes first, always.

**STOP RULE.** If at any moment you do not know what to do, or what you see differs from this sheet or the numbers message: (1) if a key is alive, do the card now; (2) then FREEZE and tell the lead in words. A paid key left alive costs money, so doing nothing is never safe while a key is alive. If two rules disagree: the card first, then R1, then R10, then the rest.
