## Appendix K: the detail of the key steps
The owner reads key steps A to K in step 22 item 3 of his sheet. This appendix is a separate page, not part of the sheet: the studio lead saves it as its own file `OWNER-APPENDIX-K.md` (the builder's copy is `r18-OWNER-APPENDIX-K.md`) next to the working copy of OWNER-STEPS and links it from step 22 item 3 by that name, so that the sheet itself stays short (order L-LEN). Where a key step says "Detail", the text is here. Nothing was deleted; the detail was moved.

K-A (detail of key step A). Why the rule is fixed: a key that nobody is prompted about could stay alive after 18:19 UTC = 23:49 IST, so every answer you give only decides whether you first make new keys for your own n8n and for other things that use the same key (key step H). The spending window ends at 18:29 UTC = 23:59 IST; the 10 minutes between go first to the key (card step 1), then to the quick web revokes of the app passwords and the HubSpot apps (card step 2), then to the shred of the key copies (card step 3); what is not finished by then you finish by Tue 24 Nov 2026 18:29 UTC = 23:59 IST.

K-C (detail of key step C). If you revoke the key because of alarm 1 and no session is open, nothing else happens until you open one: the revoked key cannot spend money, but the copy of the key, n8n's encryption key and the saved credentials stay on this laptop until the clean-up is done, so open a session with the assistant on the same day. If a session with the assistant is open when alarm 1 rings it asks "Is the key revoked?" first, but do not count on it. Time: alarm 1 gives 30 minutes. The shred steps come AFTER the revoke, so they do not compete with the deadline. If your answer to question 2 was `same key` and you did not make the new key for your own n8n before the last day, it does not fit after alarm 1 (about 30 minutes needed): revoke the key anyway; your own n8n then stops working until you put a new key in it, and the assistant lists this in the handoff. If a take is running at alarm 1, do not start key step G, part (a), until it has stopped.

K-D (detail of key step D). So, if the cut (step 21) then turns out bad, a retake needs a whole new set-up: new HubSpot apps with a new secret key, new app passwords, a new secret key for the model and a new yes (step 20). Route B: the benefit is that a retake is possible after a bad cut. The cost is that for up to five days (Wed 18 Nov to Mon 23 Nov 2026) a live paid key and its copy in the take folder stay on the disk, and so do n8n's encryption key, the credentials saved in the take's n8n (the app password of the sender mailbox, the HubSpot secret key and the password of the take's database) and the saved runs; and the revoke on the last day depends on you and your alarms (key step C). From the point of view of keeping secrets safe route A is the safer one. In route B watch the cut as soon as it is made; the deadlines of Mon 23 Nov are only the last resort, and then you have at most 10 minutes between 18:19 UTC = 23:49 IST and 18:29 UTC = 23:59 IST, and they go first to the key (card step 1), then to the quick web revokes (card step 2), then to the shred of the key copies (card step 3). If you have not written `no more takes` (in route B: `cut accepted` and `no more takes`) by 18:19 UTC = 23:49 IST on Mon 23 Nov 2026, key step D.5 applies.

K-F (detail of key step F). The assistant also asked both questions before the take (see the note at the top of Phase 0); it asks again because your answers may have changed.

K-G (detail of key step G). To shred is to overwrite the content of a file before it is removed, so that the old content is harder to recover; a plain delete (`rm`) only removes the file's name. Shredding is not reliable on every disk (flash disks and disks that keep a journal may keep the old content). The assistant's clean-up (item 1) removes the take folder only after the order of key step G, part G.2; the removal ends the path to the copy. What item 1 removes with a plain remove and not with a shred (n8n's own settings folder inside the take folder, which holds n8n's encryption key, and the take's database files) may stay in free space on the disk; whether item 1 should shred them is not decided yet and is a question for the studio lead. The password files of part (d) are listed because a plain removal by item 1 would leave their content in free space. The assistant copies the result file and the counts out of the take folder before any shred (key step E), because the password file of the take's database may be needed to read them.

K-H (detail of key step H). A new key lives only inside the program that uses it (for example the credential in your own n8n): do not save it in a file on this laptop and do not write it in a note. If you already did, tell the assistant the path (never the content) and put the path on your own list; the file is part (d) of key step G once the key is in the program that uses it.

K-I (detail of key step I). Before the take you wrote down, in your own private note, the NAME of the key and the LAST FOUR CHARACTERS of it (the Phase 0 note (under NOT YET CONFIRMED), point (2), asks you to; whether the key list shows the last four characters is UNVERIFIED; if it shows none, the note holds the name and the day you made the key). The screenshot does not prove that the right key is gone, because the assistant cannot tell from a picture which key is which.

K-8 (detail of step 8, "What the assistant can reach"). The assistant shares your Linux user, your clipboard and your screen. Once n8n runs, the assistant's programs can read n8n's encryption key (the long text n8n uses to lock its saved credentials; it is kept in files inside the take folder, and which file is UNVERIFIED) and with it can unlock the three credentials saved in n8n: the app password of the sender mailbox, the HubSpot secret key and the password of the take's database. The password is also part of the settings of the database server and n8n, which the assistant can read with ordinary commands. So use throwaway accounts and the throwaway database of this take only, and nowhere else. Never type a password inside a command line, even if the assistant or an error message suggests it as a way out. If anything asks you to, answer no.
